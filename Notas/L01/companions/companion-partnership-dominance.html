Game theory · Lecture 1 · Dominance and social dilemmas

Shirk or Work

A partnership of n people. Each chooses effort (E), at private cost c, or shirking (S). Every effort adds y to output, and output is split equally. Your own effort raises your share by y/n whatever the others do, so one comparison, y/n against c, decides dominance; a second one, y against c, decides whether the dominant choice wastes value.

Expressions: with k of the other n − 1 partners working, u(E) = y(k+1)/n − c and u(S) = yk/n, so u(E) − u(S) = y/n − c for every k (strict dominance, Tadelis Def. 4.1–4.2, pp. 60–61). All-E pays y − c to each, all-S pays 0; all-E Pareto dominates all-S iff c < y (Def. 3.5, p. 57). Social dilemma: S strictly dominant and Pareto dominated, y/n < c < y. At c = y/n, E and S pay the same against everything: neither dominates, not even weakly. At c = y, all-E and all-S tie. Landmarks are exactly reachable: y moves in steps of 0.6, so y/n is a multiple of 0.01 for n = 2…6. Defaults y = 6, c = 4, n = 2 give (E,E) = 2, (E,S) = −1, (S,E) = 3, (S,S) = 0: the slides’ prisoner’s dilemma (−2, −5, −1, −4) plus 4. Not exposed: synergy between efforts and unequal sharing (both in Listas/lista-lecture-01.tex, Q1(a) and Q2), which change the model rather than its parameters.
