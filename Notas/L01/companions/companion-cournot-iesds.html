Game theory · Lecture 1 · Iterated elimination

The Cournot Squeeze

Two firms choose quantities against linear demand p = a − bq. Round 1 of iterated elimination removes everything above each firm’s monopoly output; every later round uses the rival’s surviving interval to cut one end of your own. Step through the rounds and watch the rectangle of surviving profiles close in on the Cournot point.

Tadelis (2013) §4.2.2, pp. 65–67: p = 100 − q, ci(qi) = 10qi, eq. (4.2) q1 = (90 − q2)/2, Figure 4.1; profits p. 68. Slides: Aula/Lecture01.pdf slides 4, 28–29. Note: Notas/L01/L01-07-cournot-iesds.

a · demand intercept

The price at zero output. A bigger market raises every bound and the Cournot point.

b · demand slope

How fast price falls per unit sold. Every quantity scales with 1/b.

c1 · firm 1 marginal cost

Raises firm 1’s cost: its interval shifts down, firm 2’s up. At c1 ≥ (a + c2)/2 firm 1 is squeezed out.

c2 · firm 2 marginal cost

The same for firm 2.

k · rounds of elimination

How many levels of “I know that you know that … is rational” the firms use.

Recursion (slide 29 applied as in Tadelis pp. 66–67): Si0 = [0, ∞); Sik = [BRi(Ujk−1), BRi(Ljk−1)], with BRi(∞) = 0, BRi(q) = max(0, (a − ci − bq)/2b). Quantities below BR(U) and above BR(L) are strictly dominated because vi(x) − vi(z) = (x − z)(a − ci − b(x + z + qj)). Cournot point qi* = (a − 2ci + cj)/3b, p* = (a + c1 + c2)/3, profit (a − 2ci + cj)²/9b; if a − 2ci + cj ≤ 0, firm i produces 0 and the rival its monopoly output (a − cj)/2b. Defaults reproduce Tadelis: rounds 1–3 give [0, 45], [22.5, 45], [22.5, 33.75]; q* = 30, p* = 40, profit 900 each. The exit landmark c1 = (a + c2)/2 is on the 0.5 grid whenever a and c2 are integers (55 at the defaults); its button sets it exactly. Not exposed: more than two firms (IESDS then stops after one round, note L01-07 §6) and nonlinear costs.
