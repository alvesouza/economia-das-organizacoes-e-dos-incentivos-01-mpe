# Lecture 1 — Static games I: dominance and iterated elimination · Map of the notes

> **One claim.** **A game is a decision problem in which payoffs depend on everyone's choices**,
> written down as $G=\langle I,\{S_i\},\{u_i\}\rangle$. **Rationality alone removes strictly
> dominated strategies**, and when every player has a strictly dominant strategy the result is
> a unique, robust prediction — which can still be **Pareto dominated** (the prisoner's
> dilemma, the shirking partnership). **Common knowledge of rationality lets the removal be
> iterated**: IESDS solves games with no dominant strategies (matrix (4.1), Cournot, the
> pharmacy avenue), one level of "I know that you know" per round, and stays silent where
> nothing is ever dominated.

Three layers per section:

| Layer | What it means here |
|---|---|
| **U** — intuition | Tadelis's prose (grades on a curve, the lawyer's argument, the mafia), Gibbons's §1.1, the slides' stories (grade game, pharmacies) |
| **C** — course, examinable | the slides' definitions and propositions, with Tadelis's numbering: Defs. 3.1–3.5, 4.1–4.4, Claim 4.1, Props. 4.1–4.2; the worked games |
| **G** — formal | MWG §7.D and §8.B: order of deletion, weak elimination, the three-firm Cournot failure. **Named or sketched, not examinable** |

## The one result to hold

$$\boxed{\ \text{strictly dominant for all}\ \overset{\text{Prop. 4.1}}{\Longrightarrow}\ \text{unique DSE}\ \overset{\text{Prop. 4.2}}{\Longrightarrow}\ \text{unique survivor of IESDS (one round)}\ \overset{\text{Lesson 2}}{\not\Longrightarrow}\ \text{Pareto optimal}\ }$$

> **Each arrow adds nothing the one before did not assume**; each reverse arrow fails: IESDS can
> pick a unique profile with no dominant strategy anywhere (matrix (4.1)), and a unique DSE can
> waste value (the PD).

## Scope and ceiling

- **Examinable:** the slides' content (wrap-up, slide 34) at the level of Tadelis Ch. 3 and
  §4.1–4.2: definitions with their quantifiers, Props. 4.1–4.2 with proofs, running dominance
  and IESDS on matrices and on Cournot/location games, Pareto comparisons.
- **Named only (G):** MWG's order-independence proof (Exercise 8.B.4), weak iterated deletion,
  IESDS with infinite strategy sets, three-firm Cournot.
- **Next lecture, not here:** best responses, rationalizability (Tadelis §4.3), Nash
  equilibrium, mixed strategies and dominance by mixtures (MWG Def. 8.B.4, Prop. 8.B.1).
- **Out of course scope:** mechanism design (the second-price auction appears only as a
  dominance example), cheap talk, moral hazard (`CLAUDE.md` scope block).
