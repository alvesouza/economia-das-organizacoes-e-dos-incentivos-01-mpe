# Lecture 2 — Static games II: Nash equilibrium and mixed strategies · Map of the notes

> **Taught unit.** Built on 2026-10-16 from the study guide; reconciled on 2026-10-25 with `Aula/Lecture02.pdf`
> (slides 1–56). Notation follows the slides: payoffs $u_i$ (Tadelis writes $v_i$), mixed strategies
> $\sigma_i\in\Sigma_i=\Delta(S_i)$, Cournot benchmarks $q^m$ and $q^{cp}$, monopoly price $p^m$. Sections the lecture
> did not teach are kept as reading and flagged **"Not covered in class"**; the reconcile table is below.

> **One claim.**
> **Lecture 1 asked what a rational player would never do; Lecture 2 asks what he might do**: a best response to some
> belief, and, iterated, the rationalizable strategies.
> **Nash equilibrium adds one demand, that the beliefs be correct**: every player best-responds to what the others
> actually play, which makes the profile self-enforcing and a fixed point of the best-response correspondences.
> **With continuous strategies the equilibrium is the crossing of best-response functions**: Cournot lands between
> monopoly and competition ($q_i^*=\tfrac23q^m$), Bertrand on marginal cost (the Bertrand paradox).
> **Players can also mix, and by the principle of indifference each player's mix is pinned by the other player's
> indifference** (the Battle of the Sexes gains a third, mixed equilibrium).
> **Nash's theorem closes the loop: every finite game has an equilibrium, possibly mixed**, because mixed strategies
> make the fixed-point argument work.

## The one result to hold

$$\boxed{\;\sigma^*\ \text{is a Nash equilibrium}\iff\sigma_i^*\in BR_i(\sigma_{-i}^*)\ \ \forall i\iff\sigma^*\in BR(\sigma^*),\quad
\text{and every }s_i\text{ in the support of }\sigma_i^*\text{ earns }u_i(\sigma_i^*,\sigma_{-i}^*)\;}$$

> **Best response to correct beliefs; a fixed point; indifference on the support.** The first clause defines the
> concept, the second proves it exists, the third computes it.

## Interactive companions

- [The Mixing Square](companions/companion-mixed-2x2.html) — any 2×2 game through the four payoff differences that
  decide every best response ($\alpha_1,\beta_1,\alpha_2,\beta_2$), plus two belief probes $q$, $p$; draws both
  best-response correspondences in the $(q,p)$ square and the two indifference lines, lists **every NE (pure, mixed,
  or a continuum) exactly**, and reads out $\hat q$, $\hat p$, each player's gain at the probe and Gibbons's case.
  Default: Tadelis §6.2.3; presets Matching Pennies, Battle of the Sexes, Stag Hunt, prisoner's dilemma. Linked from
  notes 02 and 07.
- [The Cournot Crossing](companions/companion-cournot-best-responses.html) — $a$, $b$, $c_1$, $c_2$ move the two
  best-response lines; reads out $(q_1^*,q_2^*)$, price, profits, the monopoly and competitive benchmarks, the exit
  threshold and the collusion deviation. Default: Tadelis p. 87. Linked from notes 01 and 03.

## Scope and ceiling

- **Examinable (taught):** what the slides cover, at the C layer: the definitions (Tadelis's numbering), the slides'
  Props. 1–2 and the principle of indifference **with their proofs**, Nash's theorem as a statement, and solving
  matrix games (pure and mixed), Cournot (duopoly, cartel, $n$ firms) and Bertrand.
- **Reading, not covered in class:** the commons, Hotelling voting, the beauty contest, the Stag Hunt, Matching
  Pennies / RPS / the §6.2.3 game, mixed dominance and Prop. 6.2, the fixed-point proof, the cent-grid, asymmetric and
  differentiated Bertrand. Useful practice; examinable only if a problem set brings them.
- **Named only:** the index theorem (odd number of equilibria, Tadelis footnote 6); Harsanyi's purification
  (returns in Lecture 7); Gibbons §1.2.C final-offer arbitration (pp. 22–26), a NE in a game against an uncertain
  arbitrator, not written up here; the MWG G layer.
- **Out of scope** (CLAUDE.md): mechanism design, cheap talk, moral hazard. Correlated equilibrium and refinements
  (trembling-hand, MWG §8.F) are not part of this lecture.
- Exercises are cited by number and page only.
