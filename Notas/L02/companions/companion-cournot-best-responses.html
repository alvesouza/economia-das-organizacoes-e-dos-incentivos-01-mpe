The Cournot Crossing

Two firms choose quantities at the same time against linear demand P = a − bQ. Each firm’s best response falls as the rival produces more; the Nash equilibrium is the one point where each firm is on its own best-response line. Move demand and the two marginal costs and watch the crossing slide, a high-cost firm get squeezed out, and the market land between the monopoly and the competitive outcome.

Tadelis (2013) §5.2.3, eq. (5.2), printed pp. 87–88 (Fig. 5.2); §4.3.4, pp. 73–74; §5.2.4, p. 90 (monopoly benchmark); Gibbons (1992) §1.2.A, pp. 14–21

a · demand intercept

The price at which nobody buys. A higher a shifts both best-response lines out and raises every quantity.

b · demand slope

How fast price falls per unit sold. A steeper slope shrinks all quantities by the same factor; prices do not move.

c1 · firm 1’s marginal cost

Raising it pulls firm 1’s best response in and lets firm 2 expand. Above (a + c2)/2 firm 1 produces nothing.

c2 · firm 2’s marginal cost

The mirror image for firm 2.

Defaults reproduce Tadelis p. 87 and p. 90: q1* = q2* = 30, P = 40, profit 900 each; monopoly 45 units at 55, profit 2025; competitive 90 units at 10. The collusive split and the deviation read-out (Gibbons p. 17) are shown when costs are equal: each firm producing half the monopoly quantity, 22.5, is not a best reply to the other (BR = 33.75).
