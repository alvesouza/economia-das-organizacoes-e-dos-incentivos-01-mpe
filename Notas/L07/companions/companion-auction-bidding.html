The Bid Shading Desk

One bidder with value θ faces n − 1 rivals whose values are independent and uniform on [0, 1]. In a second-price auction the best bid is θ whatever the rivals do; in a first-price auction it is shaded to (n − 1)θ/n. Move your own bid and watch each format punish the deviation; move n and watch the shading shrink while the seller’s expected revenue stays the same in both formats.

Tadelis (2013) §13.1.1–13.1.4, printed pp. 272–282, Prop. 13.1, eqs. (13.1)–(13.10); Gibbons (1992) §3.2.B, pp. 155–158. Reserve price 0, risk-neutral bidders.

n · number of bidders

More rivals means a lower chance of winning with any given bid, so each bidder shades less.

θ · your value

Your private valuation (your type). Rivals know only that it is uniform on [0, 1].

Expressions: first-price bid s(θ) = (n−1)θ/n (13.8); second-price bid θ (Prop. 13.1). First-price payoff of bid b when rivals bid s(·): [min(1, bn/(n−1))]n−1(θ − b) (13.2). Second-price payoff of bid b when rivals bid their values: θbn−1 − (n−1)bn/n, i.e. E[(θ − m)·1{m < b}] with m the highest of n−1 uniform values. Expected payment of type θ: θn−1·(n−1)θ/n in both formats; seller’s revenue (n−1)/(n+1) in both (§13.1.4, pp. 279–282). Defaults: n = 2 reproduces Gibbons’s b = v/2; θ = 0.80 gives b* = 0.40, Pr(win) = 0.80, expected payoff 0.32, revenue 1/3. The “b =” buttons set b exactly (the slider shows the nearest 0.01). Not exposed: a reserve price (Tadelis exercises 13.3–13.4, pp. 285–286) and non-uniform distributions, which leave the linear closed form. Notes: Notas/L07/L07-06, L07-07, L07-08.
