# Lecture 7 — Bayesian games, adverse selection and auctions · Map of the notes

> **Estimated unit.** Built on 2026-10-16 from the study guide before the lecture (Tue Nov 17 2026). Reconcile with the slides when Aula/Lecture07.pdf arrives (/notes 07 --reconcile).

> **One claim.** **Private information is modelled as Nature drawing types** from a commonly known
> distribution, and **a Bayesian Nash equilibrium is a Nash equilibrium of type-contingent plans**.
> **When one side's type enters the other's payoff, a single price selects the wrong half of the
> market**: in Tadelis's orange grove every unit is worth more to the buyer, yet only the worst
> quality trades. **Auctions are Bayesian games with a clean answer**: bid your value in a
> second-price auction, shade to $\tfrac{n-1}{n}\theta$ in a first-price one, and **expect the same
> revenue, $\tfrac{n-1}{n+1}$, either way**. **With common values, condition on the event in which your
> choice matters** — winning, being pivotal, being accepted — or be cursed.

**Notation.** Lecture 1's slides write $G=(I,\{S_i\},\{u_i\})$; Tadelis Ch. 12 writes $N$ and $v_i$.
These notes write $I$, $u_i$, and Tadelis's types $\theta_i\in\Theta_i$ and beliefs $\phi_i$.

## The one result to hold

$$\boxed{\;s_i^*(\theta_i)\ \text{maximizes}\ E_{\theta_{-i}}\big[u_i(a_i,s_{-i}^*(\theta_{-i});\theta_i)\,\big|\,\theta_i\big]\ \text{for every type }\theta_i\;}$$

> **Every result of the lecture is this condition evaluated in one setting**: a matrix (chicken), three
> FOCs (Cournot), a cutoff (study groups), a price the seller accepts (lemons), a bid (auctions). The
> economics lives in **what the expectation conditions on** — and with common values, the event "my
> action counts" is itself information.

## Scope and ceiling

- **Examinable (C):** everything in Tadelis Ch. 12 and §13.1–13.2 used above; Gibbons's Cournot,
  linear-auction and double-auction derivations.
- **Named only (G):** MWG §8.E's formalism, §13.B's competitive equilibrium and constrained optimality;
  Harsanyi's purification theorem; the Mertens–Zamir universal type space; Myerson–Satterthwaite;
  English ≠ second-price with common values (Krishna, Milgrom).
- **Out (CLAUDE.md scope):** mechanism design — Tadelis Ch. 14, MWG Ch. 23, Gibbons §3.3 (the
  Revelation Principle). Revenue equivalence is **stated and checked for the uniform two-auction case**,
  not derived as a mechanism-design theorem; optimal reserve prices are left to Tadelis's exercises.
  Moral hazard and cheap talk are out (the grove's "my soil is good" remark is the only cheap talk here).
- Signaling and screening are the **next** lecture's subject.
