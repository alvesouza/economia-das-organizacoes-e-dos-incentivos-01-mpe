The Carrot and the Stick

A Prisoner’s Dilemma today, a Revenge Game tomorrow. The second game has two equilibria, a friendly one and a gang war, and the players can make tomorrow’s equilibrium depend on today’s outcome. Move the discount factor, the temptation and the size of the stick and watch which first-stage outcomes a subgame-perfect equilibrium can sustain.

Tadelis (2013) Ch. 9 §9.1–9.4, Prisoner-Revenge Game, printed pp. 176–184; thresholds δ ≥ 1/3 (p. 183) and δ ≥ 2/3 (p. 184) · notes: Notas/L04/L04-02

δ · discount factor

How much stage 2 counts, seen from stage 1. It scales both the carrot and the stick.

T · temptation

Payoff from finking against a mum partner (Tadelis: 5). Its excess over 4 is the gain from breaking (M, m).

S · sucker payoff

Payoff from mum against a finking partner (Tadelis: −1). 1 − S is player 2’s gain from breaking (F, m).

s · the stick, (G, g) payoff

Each player’s payoff in the gang equilibrium (Tadelis: −3). The carrot (L, l) pays 0, so the punishment is 0 − s. Above −4 it stays an equilibrium.

Held fixed at Tadelis’s values: mutual mum R = 4, mutual fink P = 1, the friendly equilibrium (L, l) = (0, 0), and the off-diagonal Revenge payoffs (−4, −1), so (L, l) stays an equilibrium and (G, g) stays one while s ≥ −4. Strategies: play the target in stage 1; play (L, l) after the target outcome and (G, g) after anything else (p. 182). (M, m) is sustained iff 4 ≥ T + δs, i.e. δ ≥ (T − 4)/(0 − s); with the book’s numbers 4 ≥ 5 − 3δ, δ ≥ 1/3 (p. 183). (F, m) is sustained iff S ≥ 1 + δs, i.e. δ ≥ (1 − S)/(0 − s); with the book’s numbers −1 ≥ 1 − 3δ, δ ≥ 2/3 (p. 184); (M, f) is the mirror image. (F, f) followed by any stage-2 equilibrium is always sustained (Proposition 9.1, p. 180). With s = 0 the stick equals the carrot and nothing but (F, f) survives (requirement 1, p. 183).
