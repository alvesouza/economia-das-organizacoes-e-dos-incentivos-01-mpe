The Patience Ledger

Two players split a pie by alternating offers; every rejection costs a period of discounting. Solve it by backward induction for any horizon, then let the horizon run to infinity. Move the two discount factors and the number of rounds and watch who the last word favours, how fast that advantage dies, and what patience alone is worth.

Tadelis (2013) Ch. 11 §11.1–11.3, printed pp. 220–229, eqs. (11.1)–(11.5) · Gibbons (1992) §2.1.D, pp. 68–71 · MWG Examples 9.AA.1–9.AA.2, pp. 296–298 · notes: Notas/L04/L04-06, L04-07

δ1 · player 1’s patience

What one period of delay leaves of player 1’s payoff. It sets what player 2 must offer player 1 in even rounds.

δ2 · player 2’s patience

What one period of delay leaves of player 2’s payoff. A more patient responder must be paid more to accept now.

T · number of rounds

Hard deadline: after round T both get 0. Odd T gives player 1 the last offer, even T gives it to player 2.

v · size of the pie

The surplus being divided. It scales the payoffs in dollars; the shares do not depend on it.

Expressions. Finite horizon with player 1 offering in odd rounds: sT = 1 if T is odd, 0 if even; going back, st = 1 − δ2(1 − st+1) when player 1 offers (t odd) and st = δ1st+1 when player 2 offers (t even). With δ1 = δ2 = δ this gives s1 = (1 + δT)/(1 + δ) for odd T, eq. (11.1) p. 226, and (1 − δT)/(1 + δ) for even T (MWG p. 297). Infinite horizon: s1 = (1 − δ2)/(1 − δ1δ2), which is 1/(1 + δ) with equal factors, eqs. (11.4)–(11.5) p. 229; undefined when δ1 = δ2 = 1 (delay is free, any split is an equilibrium). Agreement is immediate in every case (Proposition 11.3, p. 226). No book fixes a number for δ; the default 0.6 is the calibration of Aula/notes-aulas-01-02/check_part2.py, whose proposer_share verifies this page. Defaults: T = 3 gives s1 = 1 − 0.6(1 − 0.6) = 0.76; the limit is 0.625. “δ = 1 first, T = 39” shows Tadelis’s footnote 2, p. 227: take δ to 1 before T to infinity and the last mover keeps everything.
