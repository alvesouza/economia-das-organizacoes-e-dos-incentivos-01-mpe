The First-Mover Desk

The same two firms, the same demand and costs, played twice. In Cournot they choose quantities without seeing each other; in Stackelberg firm 1 commits first and firm 2 sees it. Move demand and the two costs and watch what the order of moves is worth to the leader, and what it costs the follower.

Tadelis (2013) §8.3.2, printed pp. 160–163, eqs. (8.1)–(8.2); Gibbons (1992) §2.1.B, pp. 61–64. Inverse demand P = a − bQ, constant marginal costs c1, c2.

a · demand intercept

The price at zero output. A larger market raises every quantity and profit in both games.

b · demand slope

How fast price falls per unit sold. Quantities scale with 1/b; prices and the profit ranking do not change.

c1 · leader’s cost

Firm 1’s marginal cost. Firm 1 is the leader in Stackelberg and a simultaneous mover in Cournot.

c2 · follower’s cost

Firm 2’s marginal cost. High enough, and the leader’s commitment leaves the follower nothing to sell.

Expressions. Follower: R2(q1) = max{0, (a − c2 − bq1)/(2b)}, eq. (8.1). Leader: max over q1 of (a − b(q1 + R2(q1)) − c1)q1, eq. (8.2); interior answer q1 = (a − 2c1 + c2)/(2b), q2 = (a + 2c1 − 3c2)/(4b). The leader’s profit has a kink at q1 = (a − c2)/b where firm 2 stops producing; the page evaluates the best point on each concave piece and keeps the larger (exact, no search grid). Cournot: qi = (a − 2ci + cj)/(3b), or the monopoly corner when that is negative. Defaults reproduce Tadelis p. 161: Stackelberg (45, 22.5) with profits 1012.5 and 506.25; Cournot (30, 30) with 900 each. Landmarks: equal costs (QS/QC = 9/8, Gibbons p. 63); c2 = (a + 2c1)/3, where the Stackelberg follower is shut out (40 at the defaults); c2 = (a + c1)/2, where the Cournot follower is shut out too (55 at the defaults). Not exposed: more than one follower (Tadelis exercise 8.4, p. 170) and fixed costs (exercise 8.9, p. 172).
