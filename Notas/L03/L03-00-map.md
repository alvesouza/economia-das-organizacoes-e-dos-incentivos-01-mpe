# Lecture 3 — Dynamic games: backward induction and subgame perfection · Map of the notes

> **Estimated unit.** Built on 2026-10-16 from the study guide before the lecture. Reconcile with the slides when Aula/Lecture03.pdf arrives (/notes 03 --reconcile).

> **One claim.** **When a player can see earlier moves, a strategy becomes a complete contingent plan**,
> and a game gains nodes that equilibrium play never visits. **Nash equilibrium does not check what
> players would do at those nodes**, so it admits threats nobody would carry out. **Subgame perfection
> requires a Nash equilibrium in every subgame**, reached or not, which under perfect information is
> just backward induction. **The economics is credibility**: a threat counts only if carrying it out is
> optimal when the moment comes, and **a player can gain by committing**, by moving first (Stackelberg)
> or by making one of its own payoffs worse (a bond).

## The one result to hold

$$\boxed{\;\text{SPE}\;\subseteq\;\text{NE},\qquad\text{and in finite games of perfect information}\quad
\text{SPE}=\text{backward induction}\;}$$

> **Every SPE is a Nash equilibrium; a Nash equilibrium is subgame perfect only if its plans are optimal
> at every node, including the nodes it never reaches.** Under perfect information that test is
> backward induction (Fact, p. 158); otherwise solve the last subgames, replace them by their equilibrium
> payoffs, and repeat, branching when a subgame has several equilibria.
