Game theory · Lecture 5 · Finitely repeated games

The Endgame Reward

Tadelis’s stage game has two Nash equilibria: a good one, (R, r), and a bad one, (F, f). Repeat it T times. The players cooperate on (M, m), which is not an equilibrium of the stage game, for as many rounds as they can, then play (R, r) to the end as the reward; anyone who deviates sends play to (F, f) for the rest of the game. Move the horizon, the patience and the payoffs to see how many early rounds of cooperation survive as a subgame-perfect equilibrium.

Tadelis (2013) §10.1, two-stage example and fn. 2, p. 191; Prop. 10.1, p. 192; one-stage deviation principle, Thm 9.1, p. 185 · Gibbons (1992) §2.3.A, Figs. 2.3.3–2.3.4, pp. 84–86

δ · discount factor

Weight on the next round. The reward and the punishment arrive later, so they only bite if δ is high enough.

T · number of rounds

How many times the stage game is played. T = 2 is the book’s example; longer games leave room for longer reward phases.

b · temptation

Payoff from playing F against m (book: 5). The gain from cheating in a cooperative round is b − 4.

r · carrot

Each player’s payoff in the good stage equilibrium (R, r) (book: 3). It is the reward for having cooperated.

p · stick

Each player’s payoff in the bad stage equilibrium (F, f) (book: 1). The punishment for a deviation.

Strategy profile: play (M, m) in rounds 1…n and (R, r) in rounds n+1…T; after any deviation play (F, f) to the end. Rounds with (R, r) or (F, f) prescribe a stage Nash equilibrium, so no one-stage deviation pays there. In a cooperative round t ≤ n the deviation F earns b now and p afterwards, so the profile is an SPE iff, for every t ≤ n, b − 4 ≤ Σs=t+1T δs−t(us − p), with us = 4 for s ≤ n and r for s > n (one-stage deviation principle, Thm 9.1, p. 185). The page reports the largest such n. With k reward rounds the last cooperative round needs b − 4 ≤ (r − p)δ(1 − δk)/(1 − δ): k = 1 gives δ ≥ (b−4)/(r−p), which is Tadelis’s δ ≥ 1/2 (fn. 2, p. 191); letting k grow gives the floor δ > (b−4)/(b−4+r−p) = 1/3, below which no finite game sustains any (M, m). The payoff 4 on (M, m) and the off-diagonal −1 and 0 are fixed at the book’s values; the T-round extension with a k-round reward phase is the notes’ construction, not the book’s. If r ≤ p the “carrot” is no better than the stick and nothing beyond stage equilibria survives (Prop. 10.1’s logic). This page belongs to the Lecture 5 notes L05-01 and L05-02.
