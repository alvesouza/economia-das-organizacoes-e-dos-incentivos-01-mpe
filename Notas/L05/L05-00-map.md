# Lecture 5 — Repeated Games I: Cooperation · Map of the notes

> **Estimated unit.** Built on 2026-10-16 from the study guide before the lecture. Reconcile with the slides when Aula/Lecture05.pdf arrives (/notes 05 --reconcile).

> **One claim.** **A repeated game can sustain behaviour that no one-shot equilibrium allows — but only
> if tomorrow can depend on today.** **With a known last period and a unique stage equilibrium it
> cannot:** the end unravels the whole game. **Two stage equilibria** give a carrot and a stick, so
> the early rounds can cooperate. **With no last period** the repeated game manufactures its own
> carrot — cooperation forever supports itself — and grim trigger sustains it iff
> $\delta\geq(T-R)/(T-P)$, which is $1/4$ in Tadelis's Prisoner's Dilemma. **The folk theorem then says
> almost anything individually rational can be sustained by patient players** — which explains
> cooperation as a possibility, not as a prediction.

Three layers per topic:

| Layer | What it means here |
|---|---|
| **U** — intuition | Gibbons §2.3 and Cabral §7.3: the gain-today-versus-loss-forever trade-off, $\delta$ as a continuation probability, renegotiation |

## The one result to hold

$$\boxed{\;\underbrace{R+\frac{\delta R}{1-\delta}}_{\text{cooperate forever}}\;\geq\;\underbrace{T+\frac{\delta P}{1-\delta}}_{\text{cheat once, punished forever}}
\iff\delta\;\geq\;\delta^*=\frac{T-R}{T-P}\;}$$

> **A one-time gain $T-R$ against a per-period loss $R-P$ that lasts forever.** In Tadelis's PD,
> $(5-4)/(5-1)=1/4$. In general (10.5): $\delta\geq(v_i^d-v_i^c)/(v_i^d-v_i^*)$ for every player.

*Notation.* $T$ is the PD temptation payoff in notes 04–06 and the companion, and the number of
periods in notes 01–02 (Tadelis's $G(T,\delta)$). Player 1 chooses $M$/$F$ (mum/fink), player 2
$m$/$f$, as in Tadelis and the Lecture 1 slides.
