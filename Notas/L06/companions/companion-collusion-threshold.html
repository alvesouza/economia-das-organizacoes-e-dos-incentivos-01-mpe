The Cartel Patience Dial

A cartel survives only if each member values the stream of collusive profit more than one period of cheating followed by the punishment. Choose how firms compete, how many there are and how patient they are, and read the critical discount factor, the profits behind it, and what the cartel can still sustain when patience falls short.

How firms compete

Quantities (Cournot: punishment is Cournot profit) or prices (Bertrand: punishment is zero profit).

n · number of firms

More firms shrink each share of the monopoly profit while the cheater's prize barely falls.

δ · discount factor

How much next period counts: patience, the chance the market continues, how often prices reset.

a · demand intercept

Inverse demand P = a − Q. Scales every profit; the cutoff does not move.

c · marginal cost

Constant unit cost, the same for every firm. Sets the price floor and the margin a − c.

Expressions, with A = a − c. Cournot: collusive share A²/(4n); best deviation qd = (n+1)A/(4n), profit qd²; punishment A²/(n+1)²; δ* = (n+1)²/(n²+6n+1) (9/17 for n = 2, Gibbons p. 103); below δ* the most collusive symmetric output is the smaller root of (a − nq − c)q = (1−δ)(A − (n−1)q)²/4 + δA²/(n+1)², which for n = 2 is Gibbons's q* = (9−5δ)A/(3(9−δ)) (p. 104). Bertrand: share A²/(4n), deviation takes the whole monopoly profit A²/4 (Cabral (9.3)), punishment 0; δ* = 1 − 1/n; below it every SPE prices at cost (MWG Prop. 12.D.2). Both: δ* = (vd − vc)/(vd − vp), Tadelis (10.5). Defaults are Tadelis's market (p = 100 − q, c = 10) at Gibbons's δ = 1/2: δ* = 0.529, q* = 22.94 per firm, price 54.12.
