The No-Shirking Wage

The firm cannot see effort, only output, and its one sanction is to stop employing the worker. Effort is then bought with a rent: a wage high enough that the job is worth more than the gain from shirking. Move patience, the chance that shirking goes unnoticed and the effort cost, and read the lowest wage that keeps the worker honest, and whether the job produces enough to pay it.

Gibbons (1992) §2.3.D, pp. 107–111, eqs. (2.3.5) and (2.3.7); calibration from Milgrom & Roberts (1992) Ch. 8, pp. 251–252 (USD 40,000 outside wage, USD 1,000 gain, 5% detection)

δ · discount factor

How much the worker values next period's job. M&R's multiplier N equals δ/(1−δ).

p · shirking goes unnoticed

Probability a shirker still produces high output. Detection is 1 − p; p = 0 is perfect monitoring.

e · effort cost

Disutility of effort per period: what the worker gains by shirking.

w0 · outside wage

Self-employment income, with no effort cost. The worker's fallback after being fired.

y · output with effort

What an honest worker produces. The firm pays w* only if y covers it.

Expressions (Gibbons pp. 109–110): Ve = (w − e)/(1 − δ); Vs = [(1−δ)w + δ(1−p)w0] / [(1−δp)(1−δ)]; effort iff Ve ≥ Vs ⇔ w ≥ w* = w0 + e + (1−δ)e/(δ(1−p)) (2.3.5). Panel B plots (1−δ)Ve and (1−δ)Vs.
