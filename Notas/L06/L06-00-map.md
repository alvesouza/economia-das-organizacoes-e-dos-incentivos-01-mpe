# Lecture 6 — Repeated games II: collusion and relational contracts · Map of the notes

> **Estimated unit.** Built on 2026-10-16 from the study guide before the lecture. Reconcile with the slides when Aula/Lecture06.pdf arrives (/notes 06 --reconcile).

> **One claim.** **A promise nobody can enforce in court can still be kept, if breaking it
> costs more than it pays.** In an infinitely repeated game the gain from cheating today is
> weighed against the discounted loss of the relationship, and cooperation is self-enforcing iff
> $\delta\ge(v^d-v^c)/(v^d-v^p)$. **Firms use it to collude without a contract** — $9/17$ for a
> Cournot duopoly, $1-1/n$ for $n$ Bertrand firms. **Traders use it to be trusted** — a
> reputation is that inequality held in front of many partners, and institutions lend it to
> strangers. **Firms use it to buy effort** — an efficiency wage is the rent that makes the job
> worth keeping honestly.

## The one result to hold

$$\boxed{\;\underbrace{v^d-v^c}_{\text{gain from cheating today}}\ \le\ \frac{\delta}{1-\delta}\cdot\Pr(\text{caught})\cdot\underbrace{(v^c-v^p)}_{\text{what the relationship is worth per period}}\;}$$

> **Every relational contract in the course is this line.** A cartel member, a trusted
> merchant, a guarantor and a well-paid worker differ only in what $v^c$, $v^d$, $v^p$ and the
> detection probability are.
