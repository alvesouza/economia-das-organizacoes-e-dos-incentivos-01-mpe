# Lecture 8 — Signaling and perfect Bayesian equilibrium · Map of the notes

> **Estimated unit.** Built on 2026-10-16 from the study guide before the lecture. Reconcile with the slides when Aula/Lecture08.pdf arrives (/notes 08 --reconcile).

> **One claim.**
> **Subgame perfection goes blind once types are private**: the uninformed player always moves
> inside an information set, so the only proper subgame is the whole game.
> **Perfect Bayesian equilibrium restores sequential rationality** by adding beliefs — pinned by
> Bayes' rule on the path, free off it — and asking for a best reply at every information set.
> **In signaling games** the informed player's costly action can reveal his type, but only if it
> costs the types differently; the free off-path beliefs then support **continua** of pooling and
> separating equilibria.
> **The intuitive criterion** reads a deviation as coming from whoever could gain by it, and in
> every two-type game of this lecture leaves **only the least-cost separating equilibrium**.

## The one result to hold

$$\boxed{\;\text{PBE}=\Bigl(\sigma^*,\mu\Bigr):\ \ \mu=\text{Bayes}(\sigma^*,p)\ \text{on the path},\quad
\mu\ \text{free off it},\quad \sigma^*\ \text{sequentially rational given }\mu\ \text{at every information set}\;}$$

> **Everything in the lecture is a consequence of where beliefs are free.** Free off-path beliefs
> kill non-credible threats that no belief can justify (entry game), sustain pooling equilibria
> by punishing deviations with pessimistic inferences (MBA, Spence, limit pricing), and are
> disciplined back by refinements: consistency (note 03) and the intuitive criterion (note 08).

**And one condition for any signal to work — single crossing:** the action must cost the type
that wants to be recognized *less at the margin*. In the MBA game it reads
$c_H\le w_M-w_B\le c_L$; in Spence $c_e(L,e)>c_e(H,e)$.

|---|---|---|

| MBA game | $\mu^*=\tfrac38$; $2\le4\le5$ | employer's promotion threshold; the separation constraints |
| MBA pooling | $\mu_D\in[0,\tfrac38]$ | the off-path belief that keeps everyone out of the MBA |
| Spence (M&R) | $e_H\in[1.5,3]$; $e^*=1.5$; $\bar w=29$; $p^*=0.5$ | separating set; least-cost; pooled wage; above $p^*$ H loses from signaling |
| Limit pricing | $[2.62,3.32]$ vs monopoly 2; pooling $[1.493,1.917]$ | over-production to signal low cost; pooling deters nothing |
| Fig. 15.4 | $\mu>\tfrac23$ vs consistent $\mu=\tfrac12$ | the PBE sequential equilibrium removes |
