The Education Signal

Two kinds of worker, one observable choice. Education adds nothing to output here; it works as a signal only because it costs the low type more per unit. Move the productivities, the prior and the two cost rates, and watch how much education the high type must burn to be believed, which pooling outcomes survive, and whether the high type is better off for being able to signal at all.

Milgrom & Roberts (1992) Ch. 5, “Signaling”, pp. 154–156 (the $50/$20, 30%, $10/$20 example); Gibbons (1992) §4.2.B, pp. 190–205; MWG §13.C, pp. 450–459, Fig. 13.C.8; Tadelis (2013) §16.3, pp. 332–335. Belongs to Notas/L08 (estimated before the lecture).

p · prior share of high types

Cost of education

cL · low type’s cost per unit

How much a unit of education hurts a low type. Higher cL makes mimicry dearer, so less signal is needed.

cH · high type’s cost per unit

How much a unit hurts a high type. Signaling needs cH < cL (single crossing); at or above cL nothing separates.

Model (M&R pp. 155–156; Gibbons §4.2.B with y(θ, e) = θ and c(θ, e) = cθe): competitive firms pay expected productivity, w(e) = μ(e)θH + (1 − μ(e))θL (Gibbons 4.2.1). Separating: L chooses 0 (MWG Lemma 13.C.2); H’s eH must satisfy θL ≥ θH − cLeH (L will not mimic) and θH − cHeH ≥ θL (H prefers to signal), so eH ∈ [(θH−θL)/cL, (θH−θL)/cH]. Pooling at ep with the belief “any other e means low” needs w̄ − ctep ≥ θL for both types: ep ≤ p(θH−θL)/max(cH, cL). H prefers the separating world to no signal at all iff p < p* = 1 − cH/cL (MWG Fig. 13.C.8, p. 456). The intuitive criterion (Tadelis Def. 16.1, p. 333; Gibbons Signaling Requirement 6, p. 239) keeps only the least-cost separating outcome when cH < cL. The cost is linear (M&R’s per-unit costs), so indifference curves are straight lines; the plan’s c = e/θ is the special case cθ = 1/θ. Defaults reproduce M&R: separating eH ∈ [1.5, 3] (“EH a little bit more than 1.5”), pooled wage $29, H nets $35 > $29. Landmarks: p* is exactly reachable at the defaults (0.50); cH = cL is reachable for every cL. All values are closed form; nothing is numerical.
