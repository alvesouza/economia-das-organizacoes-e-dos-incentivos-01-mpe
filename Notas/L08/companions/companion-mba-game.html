The MBA Game

Tadelis’s two-type signaling game: a worker who knows his skill chooses an MBA (D) or not (U); an employer who sees only the degree assigns him to management (M) or blue-collar work (B). Move the prior, the two study costs and the two wages; the page finds every pure-strategy perfect Bayesian equilibrium, the beliefs that hold each one up, and which survive the intuitive criterion.

Tadelis (2013) §16.1 “Education Signaling: The MBA Game”, pp. 319–323 (Fig. 16.1; cH = 2, cL = 5, wM = 10, wB = 6, p = 1/4); Requirements 15.1–15.4 and Def. 15.3, pp. 308–310; Def. 16.1, p. 333. Belongs to Notas/L08 (estimated before the lecture).

Model (Tadelis §16.1, pp. 319–323): worker’s payoff wjob − cθ·1[D]; employer’s profit from the table on p. 320 (H: M 10, B 5; L: M 0, B 3), held fixed, so the employer’s threshold μ* = 3/8 never moves. Beliefs μU, μD = Pr{H | message} follow (16.1)–(16.2) on the path; off the path any μ in the interval that makes the employer’s action a best response is allowed (Requirement 15.3). Ties count as best responses (weak inequalities). Intuitive criterion as Tadelis Def. 16.1 (p. 333) for two types: drop each type for whom the unused message is equilibrium-dominated, then ask whether a remaining type gains for sure. Defaults reproduce the book: separating (DU, BM) with μU = 0, μD = 1, and pooling (UU, BB) with μU = 1/4 and any μD ∈ [0, 3/8]; the pooling one fails the criterion (p. 334). Landmarks: p = 3/8 is exactly reachable (step 0.005); cH or cL = wM − wB is reachable on the 0.5 grid. Every value is closed form; nothing is numerical.
