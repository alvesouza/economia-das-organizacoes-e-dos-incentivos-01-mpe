The Valley Between Systems

Toyota's practices pay as a package. Start from Ford's corner, where none of them is in place, and try adopting them one at a time: each step changes profit by its own stand-alone value plus its interactions with what is already there. Set those numbers and see whether any order climbs to the full system without losing money on the way.

Each practice alone

Profit change from removing buffer stocks while machines and suppliers stay as they are.

Profit change from operators who stop the line and repair machines, with buffers still in place.

Profit change from few close suppliers instead of price competition among many.

How they interact

Extra profit when both are in place. Without buffers, breakdowns must be fixed at once.

Extra profit when both are in place. Without stocks, parts must arrive on time and fit.

Extra profit when both are in place. Shared problem solving runs through the supply chain.

Three-way term

Profit that appears only when all three are in place, beyond the three pairs.
