Talk through, in detail (2/3), the exercises of the Lecture 3 real-exercise list on backward induction and subgame perfection: solve each one step by step and stop at every point where students usually go wrong. Use the uploaded sources `Lista_Real exercises Lecture 03.pdf` (items 2, 4 and 5), `Lista_Real exercises Lecture 03 - solutions.pdf` (worked solutions and "Common mistakes"), `Aula_Lecture03 - Mixed Strategies and Dynamic Games.pdf` (slides 10–12 on indifference, 29–36 on backward induction and SPE), `Aula_Lecture04 - Credible Threats and Subgame Perfection.pdf` (slides 14–19 on subgames), `Livro_Steven Tadelis - Game Theory An Introduction (2013, Princeton University Press).pdf` (Ch. 8) and `Livro_Felix Munoz-Garcia, Daniel Toro-Gonzalez - Strategy and Game Theory Practice Exercises with Answers, 2nd ed. (2019, Springer).pdf` (Ch. 4). The listener has heard Part 1 and has the statements in hand.

The anchor: a subgame starts at a single node and contains everything after it without cutting any information set. A subgame-perfect equilibrium plays a Nash equilibrium in every subgame. Solve it by replacing each subgame with one of its equilibria, including a mixed one, and optimizing at the root.

Item 2, Muñoz-Garcia Ch. 4 Ex. 3 (sequential prisoner's dilemma). Payoffs: both confess (minus 5, minus 5); a lone confessor gets 0 and the other minus 15; both silent (minus 1, minus 1). Player 2 confesses after Confess (minus 5 beats minus 15) and after Not confess (0 beats minus 1). Player 1 confesses. The SPE is (C, CC): player 2's strategy must name a reply at both nodes. Outcome (minus 5, minus 5), as in the simultaneous game, because confessing is dominant at every node. Deeper: the Nash equilibria also include player 2 mixing off the path, playing N after N with probability w up to 5/7; only w = 0 is subgame perfect. Traps: writing the SPE as the outcome (C, C); believing player 2 "can punish" silence, when punishing is not a best reply; saying (C, CC) is the only Nash equilibrium.

Item 4, Tadelis 8.5 (adopting a technology). Inner game: the CEO chooses H or L, the manager simultaneously G or B. Payoffs (H, G) = (5, 4), (H, B) = (minus 5, 2), (L, G) = (2, minus 2), (L, B) = (0, 0). Pure equilibria (H, G) and (L, B). Mixed: the manager is indifferent when the CEO plays H with probability 1/2; the CEO is indifferent when the manager plays G with probability 5/8; payoffs (5/4, 1). Alone, this game's only subgame is itself, so all three equilibria are subgame perfect. In (b) the CEO first decides whether to adopt; not adopting gives (1, 1). Two proper subgames: the whole game and the post-adoption game. Player 2's nodes start none, since they share an information set. Pure Nash: (AH, G), (NH, B), (NL, B). SPE: one per equilibrium of the subgame. (H, G) gives 5 > 1, so adopt. (L, B) gives 0 < 1, so do not adopt, and the plan after adopting must be L: (NL, B). The mixed one gives 5/4 > 1, so adopt and mix. (NH, B) is Nash but not an SPE: after adopting, H against B is not a best reply. Lesson: adoption depends on expected coordination afterwards, and the outside option sets the bar. Traps: treating (NH, B) and (NL, B) as one equilibrium; forgetting the mixed equilibrium and its SPE; giving the simultaneous game subgames at player 2's nodes.

Item 5, Tadelis 8.10 (safe or risky). Player 1 plays safe, S, for (3, 3), or risks, R, entering a simultaneous game: player 1 C or D, player 2 A or B. Payoffs (C, A) = (8, 0), (C, B) = (0, 2), (D, A) = (6, 6), (D, B) = (2, 2). Two proper subgames. Another tree with root choices S, RC, RD gives the same reduced normal form but no proper subgame except the whole game, so the tree matters for SPE. The subgame's best replies cycle, so it has no pure equilibrium. Player 2 is indifferent when player 1 plays C with probability 2/3; player 1 is indifferent when player 2 plays A with probability 1/2. The continuation is worth (4, 2). At the root, 4 beats 3: risk. The SPE is unique and mixed, with payoffs (4, 2). The Nash equilibria also include playing safe, sustained by player 2 playing A with probability at most 1/4. Player 2 would like to commit to B but cannot. Traps: looking for a pure SPE; swapping the indifference conditions (player 1's mix comes from player 2's indifference); forgetting that (SC, B) and (SD, B) are two equilibria.

Close: count subgames by single nodes that cut no information set, solve the last subgame including its mixed equilibria, and carry every one of them back to the root.
