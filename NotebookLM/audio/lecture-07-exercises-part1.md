Talk through, in detail (1/3), the perfect Bayesian equilibrium exercises of the Lecture 7 real-exercise list: solve each one step by step and stop at every point where students usually go wrong. Use the uploaded sources `Lista_Real exercises Lecture 07.pdf` (items 1, 3 and 7, with Figure 15.5), `Lista_Real exercises Lecture 07 - solutions.pdf` (worked solutions, game trees and "Common mistakes"), `Aula_Lecture07 - Bayesian Games, Adverse Selection and Signaling.pdf` (frames 27–36) and the Tadelis book (Ch. 15). The listener has the statements in hand. Reason aloud; for every candidate, check every information set, on and off the path.

Start from the slides' entry game. Two BNE exist, (OO, F) and (EO, A). The fight threat in (OO, F) is a best reply only because player 2 never moves. Subgame perfection cannot remove it, because the only proper subgame is the whole game. The fix is the slides' Requirements 1 to 4: a belief at every information set; Bayes' rule wherever it applies; any belief off the path; and actions that are best replies to beliefs everywhere. Fighting is worse than accommodating at both nodes, so no belief supports it. That is the general test: an off-path action survives only if some belief makes it optimal.

Item 1, Tadelis 15.1 (Equilibrium Selection). Read the payoffs from the figure. C gives player 1 a sure 1. After A or B, player 2 does not know which was played and chooses L, M or R. Player 1 prefers C iff player 2 plays R with probability at most one quarter and L with probability at most one quarter, so the BNE in which player 1 plays C form a whole family, with pure (C, M) inside it. There is also a mixed BNE: player 1 plays A and B with probability one half each, and player 2 plays L and R with probability one half each. Now the PBE test. At player 2's information set with belief mu, M gives 1, while the better of L and R gives at least 2. So M is never a best reply to any belief, and the whole C family fails. Only the mixed profile, with mu equal to one half from Bayes' rule, is a PBE. Traps: "off-path beliefs are free, so M is fine" (beliefs are free, but the action must still be optimal for some belief); searching only pure profiles and concluding no PBE exists; checking SPE, which removes nothing here; missing that the C equilibria are a family.

Item 3, Tadelis 15.3 (Trust Thy Neighbor). Part (a): player 1 trusts iff player 2 would deliver, that is iff x exceeds y. In (b) Nature picks a nice player 2 (x = 1, y = 0) with probability p or a jerk (x = 0, y = 1). Stress that player 2 knows his own type, so each of his decision nodes is a singleton: no belief is needed for him, only sequential rationality. At p equal to three quarters the pure BNE are (S; D, T), (L; T, T) and (L; T, D), writing player 2's plan as (nice type, jerk). Only (S; D, T) is a PBE: in the other two the nice type would trash the problem set at a node that is off the path, which is not sequentially rational. Trust pays iff p minus (1 minus p) is at least zero, so iff p is at least one half. Part (d) is the subtle one: no value of p makes all BNE perfect, because (L; T, T) is a BNE for every p and never a PBE. Traps: drawing one information set across player 2's types; missing (L; T, D); answering (d) with "p above one half"; writing player 2's strategy as one action.

Item 7, Tadelis 15.5 (Entry again). First read the payoffs carefully. "Increases the market share by 20%" means 20 percentage points, so 60 becomes 80 and 40 becomes 60, not multiplied by 1.2. A weak entrant pays 6 to enter at any scale. A tough entrant has no exit option, so the matrix has 6 rows for the entrant, not 9. The BNE are (SX, AF), (BB, AA), (BB, FA), (BX, AA) and (BX, FA), listing the entrant's plan as (tough, weak) and the incumbent's as (after S, after B). The incumbent likes (SX, AF) best, with payoff 8, but it is not a PBE: after big entry, accommodating beats fighting against either type, so the threat to fight B is not credible even though B is off the path. In the PBE the tough type enters big, the weak type enters big or stays out, the incumbent accommodates big entry, and after small entry (off the path) it fights iff its belief that the entrant is weak is at least one half. Traps: misreading the percentages; dropping the entry cost of 6; calling (SX, AF) a PBE "because B is never played"; stating one off-path belief instead of the range that supports the response.

Close with a checklist for any PBE question: list the candidates, compute on-path beliefs by Bayes' rule, test every action at every information set against some belief, and report the belief together with the strategies.
