Teach, slowly and with many worked examples, one skill the listener finds hard: how to write a strategy and a strategy profile correctly in every kind of game of the course. Use the uploaded sources `Guide_How to write strategies.pdf` (the main source; follow its order and examples), `Livro_Steven Tadelis - Game Theory An Introduction (2013, Princeton University Press).pdf` (§7.2, Ch. 8, §10.2–10.3, §12.1–12.2, Ch. 13), `Livro_Robert Gibbons - Game Theory for Applied Economists (1992, Princeton University Press).pdf` (pp. 117–118, p. 93, §4.2.A) and the slides `Aula_Lecture03 - Mixed Strategies and Dynamic Games.pdf`, `Aula_Lecture05 - Repeated Games.pdf` and `Aula_Lecture06 - Bargaining and Bayesian Games.pdf`. Each time, say the strategy out loud in full, then say what is wrong with the usual shortcut.

Open with the one rule: a strategy is a complete contingent plan, one action at every information set the player owns, including the ones the plan itself never reaches. Separate three objects that students confuse: the strategy profile, the path actually played, and the payoffs. An equilibrium is a profile, never a path or a payoff pair.

The four-step recipe: list each player's information sets; give distinct labels to the actions at each set; fix an order and state the key ("first entry after O, second after F"); count with the product rule as a check. Explain why the plan must be complete: a best response, and subgame perfection, need to know what happens off the path.

Pattern one, the entry game from the guide: Entrant Out gives (0, 2); after In the Incumbent fights, (−1, −1), or accommodates, (1, 1). The Incumbent has a strategy even when nobody enters. Find both Nash equilibria, (Out, F) and (In, A), and show that the off-path F is what makes (Out, F) an equilibrium, a threat that is not credible. The SPE is (In, A). Mention that the leniency game in the group's short paper has this exact shape, so its SPE is written (Not propose, Accept).

Pattern two, the follower replies: the sequential battle of the sexes gives player 2 four strategies, oo, of, fo, ff, read as "after O, after F". Derive the general count, m2 to the power m1, and warn against m1 times m2.

Pattern three, a player who moves twice, from the guide: P1 Out gives (2, 0); after In, P2 plays l, (1, 2), or r, after which P1 plays a, (3, 1), or b, (0, 0). P1 has four strategies, (Out, a), (Out, b), (In, a), (In, b). Find both Nash equilibria, ((Out, a), l) and ((Out, b), l), with the same path and payoffs, and show by backward induction that only ((Out, a), l) is subgame perfect. Stress that writing "Out" alone hides the difference.

Imperfect information: one action per information set, so an unobserving follower has only two strategies. Multistage games: a strategy is a stage-one action plus a reply to every stage-one outcome, five instructions in the two-stage prisoner's dilemma, ten with a three-by-three stage game.

Mixed versus behavioral: mixing over whole plans versus mixing at each node, equivalent under perfect recall, with the conversion probability of o after O equal to p_oo plus p_of.

Repeated games: a strategy maps every history into an action. Read grim trigger in full, in Tadelis's labels: play M in stage one; afterwards play M if and only if every past outcome was (M, m), otherwise F. Give the condition delta at least (5 − 4)/(5 − 1) = 1/4. Contrast it with the wrong answer "both play M forever", which is a path. Also give tit-for-tat and the two-state machine reading.

Bayesian games: a strategy maps types to actions, one action per type, including types not drawn, because the other player needs them for expected payoffs. Use the entry game with a rational or crazy incumbent: strategies AA, AF, FA, FF, first letter the rational type. At p = 2/3, show that (E, AF) is a Bayesian Nash equilibrium. Define pooling and separating. Auctions: a strategy is a bid function, b(v) = v in the second-price auction, b(v) = v/2 in the two-bidder uniform first-price auction.

Signaling and PBE: the Sender gives one message per type, the Receiver one action per message, including a message nobody sends, and beliefs after each message, from Bayes' rule on the path and free off it. Read one PBE aloud in the guide's format, naming all three parts.

Close with the guide's mistakes, said as "the error is… the correction is…", then quiz the listener with three of the guide's self-check questions, pausing before each answer.
