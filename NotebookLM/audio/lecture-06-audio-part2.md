Explain and describe in detail (2/2) Lecture 6, "Repeated games II: collusion and relational contracts": reputation, institutions, efficiency wages and relational contracts. There are no slides yet, so follow the books. Use the uploaded sources `Livro_Steven Tadelis - Game Theory An Introduction (2013, Princeton University Press).pdf` (Tadelis §10.5), `Livro_Paul Milgrom, John Roberts - Economics, Organization, and Management (1992, Prentice-Hall, Inc.) - libgen.li.pdf` (Milgrom and Roberts Ch. 8) and `Livro_Robert Gibbons - Game Theory for Applied Economists (1992, Princeton University Press).pdf` (Gibbons §2.3.D). The listener has heard Part 1: the cutoff (10.5), Cournot and Bertrand collusion, price wars. Treat efficiency wages only as a repeated-game application; moral hazard and incentive contracts are out of scope.

The trust game: in Tadelis Fig. 10.1, no trust pays (0,0), honoured trust (1,1), abused trust (-1,2). Explain why trust fails once by backward induction, and why grim trigger sustains it iff delta is at least one half. Discuss footnote 9: "cooperate as long as I am trusted" is a Nash equilibrium but not subgame perfect, because a strategy must be optimal after every history, including those never reached.

The elementary theory: Milgrom and Roberts define relational contracts as agreements resting on good faith, assuming the parties can tell afterwards whether the right thing was done. Present their trust box with V, G and L, the decision maker's speech, and the multiplier N = 1/(1 minus delta), where delta = (1+r) to the power minus t for meetings every t periods. Derive the exact condition N V at least V + G, that is delta at least G/(V+G), and note that the book prints N V > G, which drops the round's own V. Show how meeting less often shrinks N: at 10 per cent a year, yearly meetings give N = 11 and meetings every twenty years about 1.17, and mention the multiple equilibria as the folk theorem in their words.

From relationship to reputation: the same calculation with a series of partners, provided they observe the past and can judge it. Then "live and let live" in the trenches: why a visible capacity to retaliate mattered and how raids broke the truces. Then the limits: ambiguity, outsiders who must judge, and the end game, with corporate culture (the 49ers, Nordstrom), Shasta County, CEOs near retirement, selling the business, and bankruptcy. Link the end game to finite-horizon unravelling and to Milgrom and Roberts' exercise on a trust game played twice.

Institutions: Tadelis's guarantor holds a bond of 2 for a fee of 0.1 while new traders arrive each period. Derive that he honours bonds iff delta is at least 0.95, and explain the design margins in footnote 10. Connect to the law merchant (Milgrom, North and Weingast) and Milgrom and Roberts' advanced theory: judging and broadcasting offenders, the whalers' gam, credit bureaus, and the Hansa's second-order sanction against free riding on embargoes. Then Kreps's brand names: a clean name resold above 1 acts as a bond and removes the terminal period.

Efficiency wages: set up Gibbons's stage game: a wage offer, effort cost e, outside option w0, output y with effort, and with probability p only if shirking. Derive the values of working and shirking aloud and reach (2.3.5): w* at least w0 + e + (1 minus delta) e over delta(1 minus p). Discuss the limits as p goes to 1 and delta to 0, the case p = 0, where the wage is still w0 + e/delta because tomorrow's rent is discounted, and the firm's condition y at least w*, which yields a minimum delta: efficiency is necessary, not sufficient. Discuss renegotiation, outside jobs as w0, mobility among large Japanese firms and sovereign debt. Then Milgrom and Roberts' (8.1): cheat iff g > p(w minus w-bar)N, the efficiency wage w-bar + g/(Np) and the USD 50,000 versus 60,000 example; warn that the two books use p in opposite senses. Present the minimum-cost monitoring problem, min M(p) + w subject to the incentive constraint, its first-order condition M'(p*) = g/(p* squared N), and why a longer relationship means less monitoring, then Lord Clive, the Maghribi traders and Krueger's MacRents: company-owned outlets pay supervisors 8.9 per cent more than franchised ones.

Synthesis: every case is one inequality, the gain from cheating today against the discounted, detection-weighted loss of the relationship. Lay out the design levers: raise the stake, cut the temptation, raise delta, raise detection, make judging easy, remove the end. Close with the ways relational contracts fail, and with their social sign: the levers that buy honesty from workers also keep prices high.
