One inequality: a player earns v^c per period by cooperating, v^d once from his best deviation, and v^p per period in the punishment. Derive step by step that grim trigger is subgame perfect iff delta is at least (v^d minus v^c) over (v^d minus v^p), Tadelis (10.5). Read it as temptation over temptation plus stake, and give the average-payoff form, v^c at least (1 minus delta) v^d plus delta v^p. Stress that v^d is the most tempting deviation, the static best response, not just any deviation.

Cournot collusion: work Gibbons's duopoly with P = a minus Q and cost c. Have the hosts derive the collusive profit (a minus c) squared over 8, the deviation output 3(a minus c)/8 with profit 9(a minus c) squared over 64, and Cournot's (a minus c) squared over 9, reaching delta at least 9/17. Explain why a minus c cancels, and check it with Tadelis's numbers: 1012.5 colluding, 1139.06 deviating, 900 in Cournot. Then Tadelis's unequal split, 22 and 23 units with p = 100 minus q and cost 10: the smaller firm gains more from cheating and loses less from punishment, so it needs more patience. Flag a misprint: for firm 2 the book plugs in its own output where the rival's 22 belongs; the correct deviation profit is 1156 and the cutoff 0.473, not 0.392, and firm 1's 0.595 still binds. The collusive price is 55, not 45.

Below the cutoff: explain why the Cournot cartel shrinks rather than dies. The best sustainable symmetric output is (9 minus 5 delta) over 3(9 minus delta), times (a minus c): Cournot at delta zero, half the monopoly output at 9/17. Then the n-firm cutoff, (n+1) squared over (n squared + 6n + 1).

Bertrand: walk through Cabral (9.1)-(9.4): half the monopoly profit forever against the whole of it once, so delta at least one half, and 1 minus 1/n with n firms. State MWG Props. 12.D.1-12.D.2: the condition does not depend on the price, so below one half no price above cost survives; collusion is all or nothing. Compare with Cournot: Bertrand needs less patience with two firms and more from three on. Explain why: a harsher stick against a bigger temptation.

What delta is made of: Cabral (9.5), delta = (1+g)(1-h)/(1 + r/f). Discuss each sign, the 121-firm cartel with monthly prices at 10 per cent a year, against only 11 with annual prices, and Cabral's four reasons collusion is rarer than the model predicts: antitrust, turnover, renegotiation, secret price cuts. Sharper sticks: present Abreu's two-phase strategy in Gibbons: one period of output x, then back to collusion.
