Why subgame perfection goes blind: walk through Tadelis's entry game (Fig. 15.2). Nature makes the entrant competitive with probability p or weak; the incumbent sees entry but not the type, then fights or accommodates. Build the Bayesian normal form and reason why both "everyone stays out, incumbent fights" and "only the competitive type enters, incumbent accommodates" are Bayesian Nash equilibria. Then explain why both are subgame perfect: the incumbent's two nodes form one information set, so the only proper subgame is the whole game. Add Gibbons's Fig. 4.1.1 to show the problem is non-singleton information sets, not Nature.

Beliefs and PBE: present Tadelis's Requirements 15.1 to 15.4 and Def. 15.3. A belief at every information set; Bayes' rule on the equilibrium path; any belief off it; sequential rationality at every information set given beliefs. Derive aloud the posterior p times sigma_C over p times sigma_C plus (1 minus p) times sigma_W, and say why it is 0/0 when nobody enters. Then settle the entry game: accommodating pays 1 against either type and fighting at most 0, so no belief supports the threat and the separating profile is the unique pure PBE. Stress that the work was done by having a belief and best-responding to it, not by Bayes' rule. Prove Tadelis's Prop. 15.1: a BNE reaching every information set is a PBE, because a bad action at a reached set would be a profitable ex-ante deviation. Conclude that off-path sets are the only place BNE and PBE differ. Contrast Tadelis's "weak" PBE with Gibbons's Requirement 4 (Bayes' rule where possible off the path) and say why it is vacuous in signaling games.

Sequential equilibrium: describe Tadelis's Fig. 15.4, where a PBE survives only on an off-path belief above 2/3 even though player 1 cannot see Nature's draw.
