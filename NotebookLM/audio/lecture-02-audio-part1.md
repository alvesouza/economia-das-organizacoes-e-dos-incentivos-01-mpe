Explain and describe in detail (1/2) Lecture 2, "Static games of complete information II": from beliefs to Nash equilibrium, and Cournot competition. Use the uploaded sources `Aula_Lecture02 - Static Games of Complete Information II.pdf` (the slides), `Livro_Steven Tadelis - Game Theory An Introduction (2013, Princeton University Press).pdf` (Tadelis §4.3, §5.1, §5.2.3), `Livro_Robert Gibbons - Game Theory for Applied Economists (1992, Princeton University Press).pdf` (Gibbons §1.1.C with its appendix, §1.2.A) and the MWG `Microeconomic Theory` source (§8.C, §8.D) for the formal side. The listener is an economics master's student who has heard Lecture 1 (dominance, IESDS, the prisoner's dilemma). Keep every claim precise, and reason aloud through each argument instead of listing results.

Open with the slides' question: what if no strategy is dominated? Use the Battle of the Sexes, Alex and Chris choosing opera or football: Alex gets 2 and Chris 1 at the opera together, the reverse at football, zero apart. Show that there is no dominant and no dominated strategy, so IESDS predicts nothing.

Best responses: define a best response (Tadelis Def. 4.5), the best-response correspondence BR_i, and "never a best response". Explain why BR is a correspondence: ties. State the slides' proposition (Tadelis Prop. 4.4): a strict dominant-strategy equilibrium, or a unique IESDS survivor, is a profile of mutual best responses, and walk through the proof's chain of eliminators, saying where finiteness enters. Then rationalizability: remove strategies that are never a best response, iterate under common knowledge of rationality. State Lessons 3 and 4. Point out that in the Battle of the Sexes every strategy is rationalizable, so (opera, football) survives, each player best-responding to a wrong belief.

Nash equilibrium: define it (Tadelis Def. 5.1) as a profile where no player gains from a unilateral deviation. Contrast it carefully with the epistemic concepts: IESDS and rationalizability follow from common knowledge of rationality; Nash equilibrium adds correct beliefs. Discuss the slides' three interpretations, self-fulfilling expectations, self-enforcing agreements, social conventions, and Gibbons's argument that a unique prediction must be a Nash equilibrium or it falsifies itself.

The two propositions, proved aloud. First, every Nash equilibrium survives IESDS: take the first round in which a component is removed; its rivals' components are still present, so the dominating strategy beats it against them, contradicting best response. Second, in a finite game a unique IESDS survivor is a Nash equilibrium, by the chain argument. Stress the slides' note: a Nash equilibrium may use a weakly dominated strategy, and such equilibria are considered less plausible; preview Bertrand.

Examples: the side-of-the-road game has two equilibria and IESDS removes nothing, so conventions do the selecting. The slides' prisoner's dilemma has a unique equilibrium, Pareto dominated. Mention the underline method for finding pure equilibria in a matrix.

Cournot duopoly: inverse demand P = a minus b Q, marginal cost c, outputs chosen simultaneously. Define the two benchmarks the slides use: the monopoly output q^m = (a minus c) over 2b and the competitive output q^cp = (a minus c) over b, equal to 2 q^m. Derive the first-order condition and the best response q_i = q^m minus half of q_j, zero once q_j reaches q^cp, and say why its slope of minus one half matters. Solve the two lines to get two thirds of q^m per firm, total output four thirds of q^m, price (a plus 2c) over 3. Use the slides' numbers: P = 100 minus Q, c = 10, so q^m = 45, each firm 30, price 40, profit 900. Show that IESDS reaches the same point: the bounds go one half, three quarters, five eighths, eleven sixteenths of q^m, converging to two thirds, because each round maps a bound x to q^m minus x over 2.

The cartel: each firm producing half of q^m, 22.5, earns about 1,012, but the best reply to 22.5 is 33.75, earning about 1,139. Reason through why: a firm counts only the price loss on its own units. Present the slides' two-by-two Cartel versus Deviate matrix and ask which game has the same structure: the prisoner's dilemma. Note the subtlety that the 900 cell is the payoff at the Nash outputs; if both literally produced 33.75 each would earn about 759.

Finally n firms: from q_i = 2 q^m minus Q, sum over firms to get Q = 2n over (n plus 1) times q^m, so each firm produces 2 q^m over (n plus 1); check n = 1 and n = 2, and let n grow so total output tends to q^cp and price to marginal cost.
