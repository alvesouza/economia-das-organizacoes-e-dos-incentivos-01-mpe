% Short intro shared by every driver.
\newcommand{\solintro}{\sloppy These are detailed solutions to the real book exercises of
the list \texttt{lista-reais-lectures-01-05.pdf} in \texttt{Listas/} (one block of ten per lecture). Within each lecture
the items run from easy to hard, in the list's order, and each heading shows the item's number in
the lecture, its number in the combined list, its level and its difficulty from 1 (apply a
definition) to 5 (graduate level). Statements are reproduced verbatim in the list and are not
repeated here. Every solution has the same six parts: (a) what is asked, in one line; (b) the
method, with the book section and page and the lecture slide; (c) the solution, every step shown
(for matrices, the full dominance comparisons or best-response marking); (d) the economic
interpretation; (e) the verification, by the Python check scripts in this folder and against the
book's worked answer or the MWG solutions manual where one exists; (f) common mistakes. Books:
Tadelis (2013), \MG{} \& Toro-Gonzalez (2019, 2nd ed.), Gibbons (1992), Mas-Colell, Whinston \&
Green (1995) (MWG) and Cabral (2017). Notation follows the slides: a game is
$G = (I, \{S_i\}_{i\in I}, \{u_i\}_{i\in I})$, $\sm$ is a profile of the others' strategies, $S_i^k$
is the set of player~$i$'s strategies that survive $k$ rounds of IESDS with every strictly
dominated strategy removed in each round (Lecture~1, slide~29), and $\sigma_i \in \Delta(S_i)$ is a
mixed strategy. In bimatrices player~1 picks rows and the first payoff is player~1's;
\underline{underlined} payoffs are best replies.}
