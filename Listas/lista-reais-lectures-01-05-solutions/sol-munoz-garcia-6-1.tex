\begin{exsol}{\MG{} Ch.~6 Exercise 1}{274}{Foundations}{2}
\asked In the infinitely repeated Prisoner's Dilemma of Table~6.1, can $(NC,NC)$ be an SPE outcome
when a deviation is punished by $(C,C)$ for (a) one period, (b) two periods, and (c) for ten
periods, what is the threshold $\delta$?

\method Temporary Nash reversion (``length-$k$ punishment'') and the one-stage deviation principle
(Tadelis Prop.~10.3, p.~198; grim trigger and (10.3), pp.~198--200; the general threshold (10.5),
p.~202). Compare the stream from cooperating with the stream from deviating once and then being
punished for $k$ periods; the two streams coincide from period $k+2$ on. Lecture~5 has no slides
yet; the repeated-game set-up is Lecture~4, slides~21--22. Notation as in
\texttt{Notas/L05/L05-04-grim-trigger-spe.md}.

\solution
\textbf{Notation.} Stage payoffs (player~1 first): $(C,C) = (2,2)$, $(C,NC) = (6,0)$, $(NC,C) =
(0,6)$, $(NC,NC) = (3,3)$. Present values $\sum_{t\ge0}\delta^t u_i^t$, measured from the period in
which a player considers deviating. A length-$k$ punishment strategy has two phases:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \emph{cooperation}: play $NC$ in period~1 and as long as no deviation from the prescribed
  actions has occurred;
  \item \emph{punishment}: after a deviation, play $C$ for $k$ periods, then return to cooperation
  (a deviation during the punishment restarts it).
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Step 1: the stage game.} $C$ is strictly dominant ($2 > 0$ against $C$, $6 > 3$ against
$NC$), so $(C,C)$, with $(2,2)$, is the unique stage equilibrium. The deviation gain is
$g = 6 - 3 = 3$; each punishment period costs $\ell = 3 - 2 = 1$.

\textbf{Step 2: the general condition.} Cooperating gives $3 + 3\delta + \dots$. Deviating gives
$6$ now, $2$ in each of the next $k$ periods, then $3$ forever. From period $k+1$ on (counting the
current period as 0) the streams are equal, so the comparison is over periods $0, \dots, k$:
\[
  3 + 3\sum_{j=1}^{k}\delta^j \;\ge\; 6 + 2\sum_{j=1}^{k}\delta^j
  \iff \sum_{j=1}^{k}\delta^j \;\ge\; 3
  \iff \frac{\delta(1-\delta^k)}{1-\delta} \;\ge\; 3 .
\]
In words: the one-period gain, $3$, must be covered by $k$ discounted losses of $1$ each. Since
$\sum_{j=1}^k \delta^j < k$ for $\delta < 1$, no punishment of length $k \le 3$ can ever work.

\textbf{(a) One period ($k=1$).} The condition is $\delta \ge 3$: impossible for $\delta < 1$.
Explicitly, $3 + 3\delta \ge 6 + 2\delta \iff \delta \ge 3$. Cooperation cannot be supported.

\textbf{(b) Two periods ($k=2$).} The condition is
\[
  \delta + \delta^2 \ge 3 \iff \delta^2 + \delta - 3 \ge 0 .
\]
The roots are $\delta = \frac{-1\pm\sqrt{13}}{2} \approx -2.30,\ 1.30$; the quadratic is negative on
$(0,1)$ (at most $1 + 1 - 3 = -1$). Cooperation cannot be supported.

\textbf{(c) Ten periods ($k=10$).} The condition is
\[
  \delta + \delta^2 + \dots + \delta^{10} \ge 3 .
\]
The left side rises from $0$ to $10$ on $[0,1]$, so there is exactly one root in $(0,1)$. Numerically
(bisection or a polynomial root-finder),
\[
  \delta_{10} \approx 0.7627 .
\]
Cooperation is an SPE outcome iff $\delta \ge \delta_{10}$. For comparison, the thresholds for other
lengths are $\delta_4 \approx 0.888$, $\delta_5 \approx 0.834$, $\delta_6 \approx 0.805$, $\delta_8 \approx
0.775$, $\delta_{20} \approx 0.7506$, and grim trigger ($k = \infty$) needs $\frac{\delta}{1-\delta} \ge 3$,
i.e.\ $\delta \ge \frac34$ ((10.5): $\frac{6-3}{6-2} = \frac34$).

\textbf{Step 3: the punishment phase.} In a punishment period the prescribed action is $C$, a stage
best reply to $C$. Deviating to $NC$ pays $0 < 2$ today and either leaves the future unchanged or,
with the restart rule, lengthens the punishment, which pays $2 < 3$ per period. Either way the
deviation loses, for every $\delta$. So the only binding constraint is Step~2's, and the profile is an
SPE exactly when that holds.

\interp A punishment deters only if the deviator loses more than he grabs. Here the grab is $3$
and each period of punishment costs only $1$, so at least four periods are needed, and even then only
very patient players are deterred. Longer punishments lower the threshold, with grim trigger as the
limit. Short, forgiving punishments are attractive (mistakes are not punished forever), but they
need a punishment phase that hurts much more per period than reverting to the Nash equilibrium does
here.

\verify \texttt{check\_C\_L05.py} confirms that $(C,C)$ is the unique stage equilibrium and derives
the condition $\sum_{j=1}^k\delta^j \ge 3$ symbolically for $k = 1, 2, 3, 4, 10$. It shows (a), (b),
and also $k = 3$ fail on the grid $\delta = j/1000$. For (c) it finds the single root in $(0,1)$,
$0.762700984\ldots$, and brackets it in exact fractions: the condition fails at $0.762700$, holds at
$0.762701$, fails at $0.76$ and holds at $0.77$. It also checks that the thresholds decrease in $k$
towards $\frac34$. Output: ``Munoz-Garcia 6.1: k=1 and k=2 never work (k=3 needs delta=1); k=10:
delta >= 0.762701 (book: 0.76, which fails); grim: 3/4; k=4: 0.8882''.
\emph{Comparison with \MG{}'s answer} (pp.~275--278): the same conditions and conclusions for (a)
and (b) ($\delta \ge 3$; $\delta^2 + \delta - 3 \ge 0$ with roots $-2.3$ and $1.3$). For (c) the book
reports $\delta \ge 0.76$, which is the threshold rounded \emph{down}: at $\delta = 0.76$ the sum is
about $2.963 < 3$, so cooperation fails. The correct statement is $\delta \ge 0.7627$. Two slips in
the book's text for (a): it says the punisher finds ``$NC$ a best response to player~$i$ playing
$NC$'', meaning $C$ against $C$; and it calls $(NC,NC)$ the ``competitive outcome'' where it means the
cooperative one. The book does not check the punishment phase.

\mistakes
\begin{itemize}
  \item Calling these strategies ``tit-for-tat''. True tit-for-tat copies the opponent's last move,
  which is not the same as ``both play $C$ for $k$ periods''. Tit-for-tat proper is generally
  \emph{not} subgame perfect, because after a deviation it requires a player to punish while being
  punished. The book uses the name loosely.
  \item Comparing the streams only up to period~1 (``$6 + 2\delta$ versus $3 + 3\delta$'') when
  $k > 1$. All $k$ punishment periods enter the comparison.
  \item Rounding the threshold to $0.76$. A threshold must be rounded up, or stated as the exact
  root.
\end{itemize}

\answer{With a $k$-period reversion to $(C,C)$, cooperation is an SPE iff $\sum_{j=1}^{k}\delta^j\ge3$
(the punishment phase never binds). (a) $k=1$: $\delta\ge3$, impossible. (b) $k=2$: $\delta^2+\delta-3\ge0$,
root $\frac{-1+\sqrt{13}}2\approx1.30>1$, impossible. (c) $k=10$: $\delta+\delta^2+\dots+\delta^{10}\ge3$,
i.e.\ $\delta\ge\delta_{10}\approx0.7627$ (the book's $0.76$ is slightly too low). Grim trigger would need
$\delta\ge\frac34$.}
\end{exsol}
