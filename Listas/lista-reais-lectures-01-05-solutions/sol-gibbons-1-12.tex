\begin{exsol}{Gibbons Problem 1.12}{51}{Exam}{2}
\asked Find the mixed-strategy Nash equilibrium of the $2\times2$ game with $T$, $B$ against
$L$, $R$.

\method Mixed strategies and expected payoffs (Gibbons \S1.3.A, pp.~29--33; Lecture~2,
slides~42--43); mixed Nash equilibrium through the principle of indifference (Gibbons \S1.3.B,
pp.~33--44; Tadelis Prop.~6.1, p.~108; Lecture~2, slides~44--46). First check that there is no pure
equilibrium, so any equilibrium must have both players mixing.

\solution
\textbf{Best responses}, underlined:
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{r|c|c|}
\multicolumn{1}{r}{1 $\backslash$ 2} & \multicolumn{1}{c}{$L$} & \multicolumn{1}{c}{$R$}\\ \cline{2-3}
$T$ & $\br{2},\ 1$ & $0,\ \br{2}$ \\ \cline{2-3}
$B$ & $1,\ \br{2}$ & $\br{3},\ 0$ \\ \cline{2-3}
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
$BR_1(L) = \{T\}$ ($2 > 1$), $BR_1(R) = \{B\}$ ($3 > 0$); $BR_2(T) = \{R\}$ ($2 > 1$),
$BR_2(B) = \{L\}$ ($2 > 0$). No cell has both payoffs underlined: the best responses cycle
$(T,L) \to (T,R) \to (B,R) \to (B,L) \to (T,L)$, so there is no pure-strategy Nash equilibrium.

\textbf{No equilibrium with only one player mixing.} If player~2 played a pure strategy, player~1's
best reply would be unique and pure, and then player~2's best reply to it would be the other
column; so in equilibrium player~2 mixes, and by the same argument so does player~1. Both must be
indifferent between their two pure strategies (Lecture~2, slide~44).

\textbf{Player~2's mix, from player~1's indifference.} Let $q = \Pr(L)$.
\[
  EU_1(T) = 2q + 0(1-q) = 2q, \qquad EU_1(B) = 1\cdot q + 3(1-q) = 3 - 2q .
\]
\[
  2q = 3 - 2q \;\Longrightarrow\; 4q = 3 \;\Longrightarrow\; q^* = \tfrac34 .
\]
\textbf{Player~1's mix, from player~2's indifference.} Let $p = \Pr(T)$.
\[
  EU_2(L) = 1\cdot p + 2(1-p) = 2 - p, \qquad EU_2(R) = 2p + 0(1-p) = 2p .
\]
\[
  2 - p = 2p \;\Longrightarrow\; 3p = 2 \;\Longrightarrow\; p^* = \tfrac23 .
\]
\textbf{Equilibrium and payoffs.}
\[
  \sigma^* = \Big(\big(\tfrac23\,T,\ \tfrac13\,B\big),\ \big(\tfrac34\,L,\ \tfrac14\,R\big)\Big),
  \qquad u_1^* = 2q^* = \tfrac32, \qquad u_2^* = 2p^* = \tfrac43 .
\]
\textbf{Uniqueness.} $EU_1(T) - EU_1(B) = 4q - 3$, so $BR_1(q)$ is $p = 1$ for $q > \tfrac34$,
$p = 0$ for $q < \tfrac34$, and $[0,1]$ at $q = \tfrac34$. $EU_2(L) - EU_2(R) = 2 - 3p$, so
$BR_2(p)$ is $q = 1$ for $p < \tfrac23$, $q = 0$ for $p > \tfrac23$, and $[0,1]$ at
$p = \tfrac23$. The graphs cross only at $(p,q) = (\tfrac23, \tfrac34)$: it is the unique Nash
equilibrium.

\interp This is a Matching-Pennies-like game (Gibbons \S1.3.A): player~1 wants to match
($T$ with $L$, $B$ with $R$), player~2 wants to mismatch. Each player randomises so as to leave the
\emph{other} indifferent, which is why player~1's probabilities come from player~2's payoffs. For
instance, raising player~1's payoff at $(B,R)$ from $3$ to $4$ would leave $p^*$ unchanged and
raise $q^*$ to $\tfrac45$ (from $2q = 4 - 3q$).

\verify \texttt{check\_A2.py} confirms by brute force that there is no pure equilibrium, solves both
indifference conditions with sympy ($q^* = \tfrac34$, $p^* = \tfrac23$), checks the payoffs
$\tfrac32$ and $\tfrac43$ and the comparative-statics remark, and finds on a grid of mixed profiles
that $(\tfrac23, \tfrac34)$ is the only mutual best response. Gibbons gives no answer.

\mistakes
\begin{itemize}
  \item Solving player~1's indifference for $p$. The condition $EU_1(T) = EU_1(B)$ contains only
  $q$, player~2's probability.
  \item Reporting $(\tfrac34, \tfrac23)$ in the wrong order. State which probability belongs to
  which player and which strategy.
  \item Stopping at ``no pure NE''. Nash's theorem (Lecture~2, slide~51) guarantees a mixed one, and
  the question asks for it.
\end{itemize}

\answer{No pure-strategy equilibrium. The unique (mixed) Nash equilibrium: player~1 plays $T$ with
probability $\tfrac23$ and $B$ with $\tfrac13$; player~2 plays $L$ with probability $\tfrac34$ and
$R$ with $\tfrac14$. Expected payoffs $\tfrac32$ for player~1 and $\tfrac43$ for player~2.}
\end{exsol}
