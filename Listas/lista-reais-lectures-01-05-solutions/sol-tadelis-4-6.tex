\begin{exsol}{Tadelis Exercise 4.6}{77}{Exam}{4}
\asked For cleaning times $t_i \ge 0$ with payoff $(10 - t_j)t_i - t_i^2$, find the best response,
the survivors of one round of IESDS, and the survivors of IESDS.

\method Best response (Lecture~2, slide~10; Tadelis \S4.3.1, p.~69) and IESDS with a continuum of
strategies (Lecture~1, slide~29), done exactly as in Tadelis's Cournot example (\S4.2.2,
pp.~65--67, Fig.~4.1) and in the slides' Cournot remark (Lecture~2, slide~24): bound the set of
possible best replies and show that every action outside it is strictly dominated by the nearest
end of the range.

\solution
\textbf{(a) Best response.} Fix $t_j \ge 0$ and maximise $u_i(t_i, t_j) = (10 - t_j)t_i - t_i^2$
over $t_i \ge 0$.
\begin{itemize}
  \item $\partial u_i/\partial t_i = 10 - t_j - 2t_i$ and $\partial^2 u_i/\partial t_i^2 = -2 < 0$:
  $u_i$ is strictly concave, so the first-order condition with the constraint $t_i \ge 0$ gives the
  unique maximiser.
  \item If $t_j < 10$, the FOC $10 - t_j - 2t_i = 0$ gives $t_i = (10 - t_j)/2 > 0$.
  \item If $t_j \ge 10$, $\partial u_i/\partial t_i = 10 - t_j - 2t_i < 0$ for every $t_i > 0$, so
  the maximum is at the corner $t_i = 0$.
\end{itemize}
\[
  BR_i(t_j) = \max\Big\{0,\ 5 - \frac{t_j}{2}\Big\}.
\]
It is single-valued and decreasing in $t_j$: cleaning is a strategic substitute.

\textbf{Dominance lemma.} Write $x = (10 - t_j)/2$ for the unconstrained peak, so
$u_i(t, t_j) = 2xt - t^2 = t(2x - t)$. For any two times $a$ and $t$,
\[
  u_i(a, t_j) - u_i(t, t_j) = 2x(a - t) - (a^2 - t^2) = (a - t)(2x - a - t)
  = (t - a)(t + a - 2x). \tag{1}
\]
If $t > a \ge x$, both factors on the right are positive; if $t < a \le x$, both are negative. So:
if every peak $x$ that the opponent's surviving strategies can generate lies in
$[\underline a, \bar a]$, then every $t > \bar a$ is strictly dominated by $\bar a$ and every
$t < \underline a$ by $\underline a$. Conversely, a $t$ that is the unique best reply to some
surviving $t_j$ is never strictly dominated.

\textbf{(b) One round.} With $t_j \in [0, \infty)$, the best replies range over $[0, 5]$
($BR_i(0) = 5$, $BR_i(t_j) = 0$ for $t_j \ge 10$), and the peaks $x = 5 - t_j/2$ are all
$\le 5$.
\begin{itemize}
  \item Every $t_i > 5$ is strictly dominated by $5$: by (1) with $a = 5 \ge x$, for every $t_j$
  (if $x < 0$ the inequality still holds because $t + a - 2x > 0$).
  \item Every $t_i \in [0, 5]$ is the unique best reply to $t_j = 10 - 2t_i \in [0, 10]$, so it is
  not dominated.
\end{itemize}
Hence $S_i^1 = [0, 5]$ for both roommates.

\textbf{(c) Iterating.} Suppose $S_j^k = [L_k, U_k]$. Because $BR_i$ is continuous and decreasing,
the best replies to $t_j \in [L_k, U_k]$ fill exactly $[BR_i(U_k), BR_i(L_k)]$; by (1) everything
outside that interval is strictly dominated by its nearer endpoint, and everything inside is a best
reply. So
\[
  L_{k+1} = 5 - \frac{U_k}{2}, \qquad U_{k+1} = 5 - \frac{L_k}{2}, \qquad (L_1, U_1) = (0, 5).
\]
(The $\max\{0,\cdot\}$ never binds from round~2 on because $U_k \le 5 < 10$.)
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{c|cccccc}
$k$ & 1 & 2 & 3 & 4 & 5 & $\infty$ \\ \hline
$L_k$ & $0$ & $5 - 5/2 = 2.5$ & $2.5$ & $5 - 3.75/2 = 3.125$ & $3.125$ & $10/3$ \\
$U_k$ & $5$ & $5 - 0/2 = 5$ & $5 - 2.5/2 = 3.75$ & $3.75$ & $5 - 3.125/2 = 3.4375$ & $10/3$
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
\emph{Convergence.} Subtracting the two recursions,
$U_{k+1} - L_{k+1} = \tfrac12 (U_k - L_k)$, so $U_k - L_k = 5/2^{k-1} \to 0$. Adding them, the
midpoint $M_k = (L_k + U_k)/2$ satisfies $M_{k+1} = 5 - M_k/2$. Its fixed point solves
$M = 5 - M/2$, i.e.\ $M = 10/3$, and
\[
  M_k - \tfrac{10}{3} = \big(-\tfrac12\big)^{k-1}\big(M_1 - \tfrac{10}{3}\big)
  = \big(-\tfrac12\big)^{k-1}\big(\tfrac52 - \tfrac{10}{3}\big)
  = \big(-\tfrac12\big)^{k-1}\big(-\tfrac56\big) \to 0 .
\]
The intervals are nested, their widths go to zero and their midpoints go to $10/3$, so
$S_i^\infty = \bigcap_k S_i^k = \{10/3\}$ for both roommates. IESDS predicts the unique profile
$t_1 = t_2 = 10/3$. It is also the Nash equilibrium: $t = 5 - t/2$ gives $t = 10/3$.

\interp Each round uses one more layer of knowledge: round~1, ``I am rational'' (never clean more
than 5 hours); round~2, ``I know you are rational'' (you clean at most 5, so I clean at least
2.5); and so on (Lecture~2, slide~7). Each roommate gets
$u_i = (10 - 10/3)(10/3) - (10/3)^2 = 200/9 - 100/9 = 100/9 \approx 11.1$. Joint welfare
$2[(10 - t)t - t^2] = 2(10t - 2t^2)$ is maximised at $10 - 4t = 0$, i.e.\ $t = 2.5$, where each
gets $(7.5)(2.5) - 6.25 = 12.5 > 100/9$. The IESDS outcome over-cleans relative to the joint
optimum: each roommate ignores that his cleaning lowers the value of the other's (a negative
externality, $\partial u_j/\partial t_i = -t_j < 0$).

\verify \texttt{check\_A1.py} checks the best reply and the factorization (1) with sympy, runs the
interval recursion for 40 rounds in exact arithmetic (the table, width $5/2^{k-1}$ and the
midpoint formula at every round), checks the payoffs $100/9$ and $12.5$ and the joint optimum
$2.5$, and, as a second route, runs brute-force IESDS on the grid $\{0, \tfrac16, \dots, 10\}$,
which leaves exactly $t_1 = t_2 = 10/3$. Tadelis gives no answer.

\mistakes
\begin{itemize}
  \item Answering (b) with $[0, \infty)$ minus nothing, or with $[0, 10]$. The bound comes from
  the best replies, which never exceed $5$, not from where the payoff is positive.
  \item Writing $L_{k+1} = 5 - L_k/2$: the \emph{upper} bound of the opponent gives the
  \emph{lower} bound of the best reply, because $BR_i$ is decreasing.
  \item Claiming that a $t$ outside the best-reply range is ``dominated'' without naming the
  dominating strategy. Lemma (1) supplies it: the nearer endpoint.
\end{itemize}

\answer{(a) $BR_i(t_j) = \max\{0, 5 - t_j/2\}$. (b) $S_i^1 = [0, 5]$: any $t_i > 5$ is strictly
dominated by $5$. (c) Rounds give $[2.5, 5]$, $[2.5, 3.75]$, $[3.125, 3.75]$, $[3.125, 3.4375]$,
\dots, with widths $5/2^{k-1}$; only $t_1 = t_2 = 10/3$ survives IESDS.}
\end{exsol}
