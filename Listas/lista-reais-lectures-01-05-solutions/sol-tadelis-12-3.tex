\begin{exsol}{Tadelis Exercise 12.3}{266--267}{Exam level}{3}
\asked Two armies, each privately strong ($S$) or weak ($W$) with probability $\frac12$,
independently, choose to attack ($A$) or not ($N$). Find all pure-strategy BNE for (a) $m = 3$,
$w = 2$, $s = 1$ and (b) $m = 3$, $w = 4$, $s = 2$.

\method Bayesian game with two-sided private information (Lecture~6, frame~28) and the recipe of
frame~36: for each \emph{type} of each army, find the best action against each possible strategy of
the rival; then keep the profiles in which every type best-responds. A strategy is a pair
$(x_S, x_W)$: action of the strong type, action of the weak type (frame~29). Types are independent, so
every type believes the rival is strong with probability $\frac12$. Book: Tadelis \S12.1 (pp.~246--252),
\S12.2 (examples, pp.~252--258).

\solution
\textbf{Payoffs of one army.} $N$ gives $0$ (no capture, no fight). $A$ gives: $m$ if the rival plays
$N$ (no fight, no cost); if both attack, the army pays its fighting cost $c \in \{s, w\}$ and captures
the territory only when it is strong and the rival weak. So, for an attacking army of type $t$ facing an
attacking rival of type $t'$:
\[
  u(A \mid t, t') = \begin{cases} m - s & t = S,\ t' = W,\\ -s & t = S,\ t' = S,\\ -w & t = W
  \ (\text{any } t'). \end{cases}
\]
\textbf{Expected payoff of $A$.} Against a rival strategy $(y_S, y_W)$, type $t$ gets
$E[A] = \frac12 u(A \mid t, \text{strong rival plays } y_S) + \frac12 u(A \mid t, \text{weak rival plays }
y_W)$, where a rival playing $N$ yields $m$. Since $N$ yields $0$, type $t$ attacks iff $E[A] \ge 0$.

\textbf{(a) $m = 3$, $w = 2$, $s = 1$.} Strong: $-1$ vs an attacking strong rival, $2$ vs an attacking
weak rival, $3$ vs a passive rival. Weak: $-2$ vs any attacking rival, $3$ vs a passive one.
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{l|cccc}
rival $(y_S, y_W)$ & $AA$ & $AN$ & $NA$ & $NN$\\\hline
strong: $E[A]$ & $\frac12(-1)+\frac12(2) = \frac12$ & $\frac12(-1)+\frac12(3) = 1$ & $\frac12(3)+\frac12(2) = \frac52$ & $3$\\
weak: $E[A]$ & $-2$ & $\frac12(-2)+\frac12(3) = \frac12$ & $\frac12(3)+\frac12(-2) = \frac12$ & $3$\\\hline
best reply & $AN$ & $AA$ & $AA$ & $AA$
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
The strong type always attacks; the weak type attacks unless the rival always attacks. Look for
mutual best replies: $BR(AA) = AN$ and $BR(AN) = AA$, so
\[
  (AA,\ AN) \quad\text{and}\quad (AN,\ AA)
\]
are BNE. No symmetric profile works: $BR(AA) = AN \ne AA$ and $BR(AN) = AA \ne AN$; $NA$ and $NN$ are
never best replies. Check $(AA, AN)$ type by type: army~1 faces $AN$, strong $1 > 0$ and weak
$\frac12 > 0$, so both attack; army~2 faces $AA$, strong $\frac12 > 0$ attacks and weak $-2 < 0$ stays.

\textbf{(b) $m = 3$, $w = 4$, $s = 2$.} Strong: $-2$ vs an attacking strong rival, $1$ vs an attacking
weak rival, $3$ vs a passive rival. Weak: $-4$ vs any attacking rival, $3$ vs a passive one.
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{l|cccc}
rival $(y_S, y_W)$ & $AA$ & $AN$ & $NA$ & $NN$\\\hline
strong: $E[A]$ & $\frac12(-2)+\frac12(1) = -\frac12$ & $\frac12(-2)+\frac12(3) = \frac12$ & $\frac12(3)+\frac12(1) = 2$ & $3$\\
weak: $E[A]$ & $-4$ & $\frac12(-4)+\frac12(3) = -\frac12$ & $\frac12(3)+\frac12(-4) = -\frac12$ & $3$\\\hline
best reply & $NN$ & $AN$ & $AN$ & $AA$
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
Mutual best replies: $BR(AN) = AN$; $BR(AA) = NN$ and $BR(NN) = AA$. So
\[
  (AN,\ AN), \qquad (AA,\ NN), \qquad (NN,\ AA).
\]
$NA$ is never a best reply. Check $(AA, NN)$: army~1 faces a rival that never attacks, so both its
types get $3 > 0$ from $A$; army~2 faces a rival that always attacks, strong $-\frac12 < 0$ and weak
$-4 < 0$, so both stay out.

\interp In (a) fighting is cheap relative to the prize, so even a weak army attacks unless it is sure to
meet an attacker; equilibrium needs an asymmetry in which one army's weak type backs off. In (b)
fighting is expensive: the weak type attacks only an army that never attacks, and the natural
symmetric outcome is ``only the strong fight'' $(AN, AN)$; the lopsided $(AA, NN)$, where one army is
``known'' to be aggressive and the other always yields, also survives. As in Munich (frames~23--27),
each type best-responds to the rival's whole plan, weighted by its belief about the rival's type.

\verify \texttt{check\_F\_L06.py} derives each army's payoffs from the rules in the statement, builds
both $4\times 4$ Bayesian normal forms (each of the four type pairs with probability $\frac14$) and
enumerates the sixteen pure profiles. Output: ``Tadelis 12.3: (a) BNE = (AA,AN), (AN,AA); (b) BNE =
(AA,NN), (AN,AN), (NN,AA)''. Tadelis gives no answer.

\mistakes
\begin{itemize}
  \item Giving the weak army $m - w$ when it attacks a weak attacker. If both attack with equal strength,
  \emph{neither} captures: the payoff is $-w$.
  \item Charging the fighting cost when only one army attacks. If the rival does not attack there is no
  fight: the attacker gets $m$, the passive army $0$.
  \item Treating the strong army's expected payoff as if the rival's \emph{action} were fixed. The rival's
  strong and weak types may act differently, so $E[A]$ depends on the whole rival strategy $(y_S,y_W)$.
  \item Looking only for symmetric equilibria. In (a) there is none; both BNE are asymmetric.
  \item Using the posterior ``the rival is like me''. Types are independent: whatever its own type, an army
  puts probability $\frac12$ on the rival being strong.
\end{itemize}

\answer{Strategies list (strong type, weak type). (a) $(AA, AN)$ and $(AN, AA)$: the strong type always
attacks; one army's weak type attacks and the other's stays out. (b) $(AN, AN)$, $(AA, NN)$ and
$(NN, AA)$.}
\end{exsol}
