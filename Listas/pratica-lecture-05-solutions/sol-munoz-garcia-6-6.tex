\begin{exsol}{\MG{} Ch.~6 Exercise 6}{292--293}{Exam level}{3}
\asked In a two-player public-good game with $u_i = \sqrt{(w - g_i)\,m\,(g_i + g_j)}$: find the best
responses, the Nash equilibrium and the socially optimal contribution, then the discount factors for which
grim trigger sustains the socially optimal contribution in the infinitely repeated game.

\method Best responses and the stage Nash equilibrium, then grim trigger with the one-stage deviation
principle and normalised payoffs (Lecture~5, slides~21, 24--29; Tadelis \S10.3, pp.~196--202). The
incentive constraint compares the cooperative stage payoff with the best deviation followed by the stage
Nash payoff forever.

\solution
\textbf{(a) Best response.} Squaring does not change the maximiser, so player~$i$ maximises
$(w - g_i)(g_i + g_j)$: $\;(w - g_i) - (g_i + g_j) = 0$, hence
\[
  g_i(g_j) = \frac{w - g_j}{2} = \frac w2 - \frac12\,g_j .
\]
A rival's contribution crowds out one half of one's own: the response is $\frac w2$ when $g_j = 0$ and falls
to $0$ at $g_j = w$. The second-order condition holds, since $(w-g_i)(g_i+g_j)$ is concave in $g_i$.

\textbf{(b) Nash equilibrium.} Symmetric: $g = \frac{w - g}{2}$, so $g^* = \frac w3$. Payoff
$u_i^* = \sqrt{\tfrac{2w}{3}\cdot m\cdot\tfrac{2w}{3}} = \frac{2w}{3}\sqrt m$.

\textbf{(c) Social optimum.} Maximise $u_A + u_B$. At a symmetric profile each term is
$\sqrt{(w - g)\,2mg}$, so the joint problem is to maximise $(w - g)g$, with
solution $g^{SO} = \frac w2$ (the first-order condition for the asymmetric problem is also satisfied at
$g_A = g_B = \frac w2$). Payoff $u_i^{SO} = \sqrt{\tfrac w2\cdot m\cdot w} = \frac{w\sqrt m}{\sqrt2} \approx 0.707\,w\sqrt m$,
against $u_i^* = 0.667\,w\sqrt m$: the difference is $\frac{3\sqrt2 - 4}{6}\,w\sqrt m \approx 0.04\,w\sqrt m > 0$.
Each player is better off at the social optimum, which has a larger contribution ($\frac w2 > \frac w3$):
at Nash, each ignores the benefit his donation gives the other and free-rides.

\textbf{(d) Grim trigger.} Strategy: contribute $\frac w2$ as long as both have; otherwise contribute $\frac w3$
forever. Per-period payoffs, in units of $w\sqrt m$:
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{lll}
\toprule
cooperation & $\frac{1}{\sqrt2}$ & both give $\frac w2$ \\
best deviation & $\frac34$ & $g_i = \frac{w - w/2}{2} = \frac w4$ against $g_j = \frac w2$:
 $\sqrt{\tfrac{3w}{4}\,m\,\tfrac{3w}{4}} = \frac{3w}{4}\sqrt m$ \\
punishment (Nash) & $\frac23$ & both give $\frac w3$ \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
The punishment state needs no check (a stage equilibrium). The cooperative state requires, in
normalised payoffs,
\[
  \frac{1}{\sqrt2} \;\ge\; (1-\delta)\,\frac34 + \delta\,\frac23
  \iff \delta \;\ge\; \frac{\frac34 - \frac1{\sqrt2}}{\frac34 - \frac23}
  = 12\Bigl(\frac34 - \frac{1}{\sqrt2}\Bigr) = 9 - 6\sqrt2 \approx 0.5147 .
\]
This is (10.5), $\delta^* = (v^d - v^c)/(v^d - v^*)$. The threshold does not depend on $w$ or $m$, because both
scale every payoff by the same factor.

\interp Cooperation is worth only $0.04$ more than Nash (a $6\%$ gain) while a deviation pays $0.043$
more than cooperating. The two are of the same size, so the players must be fairly patient, $\delta \ge 0.51$.
