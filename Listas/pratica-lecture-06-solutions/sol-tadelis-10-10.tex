\begin{exsol}{Tadelis Exercise 10.10}{219}{Advanced}{4}
\asked In the three-person game of Section~10.5.2 (a bond $B = 2$ and a fee $w = 0.1$ sustain cooperation for
$\delta \ge 0.95$), show that a similar equilibrium (trust, bond, cooperation, guarantor honest in every period)
exists for any discount factor $0 < \delta < 1$.

\method The structure of \S10.5.2 (pp.~205--207). The trust game of Figure~10.1: no trust $(0,0)$; trust and
cooperate $(1,1)$; trust and defect $(-1, 2)$ for players $(P_1^t, P_2^t)$. With a bond $B$ posted with player~3,
a defector loses $B$ (Figure~10.2 uses $B = 2$). The guarantor (player~3) lives for ever and discounts at $\delta$.
Footnote~10 (p.~207) says the bond need only exceed $1$ and that $P_2^t$ is willing to pay any fee up to $1$. We choose
$B$ and $w$ and verify three incentive constraints, using the book's conventions: $P_2^t$ pays the fee in every
period in which the guarantor follows the contract (including after his own defection, when the guarantor keeps
the bond), and the guarantor's alternative is to keep the bond after a cooperation, which earns $B$ in that period
and ends all future business.

\solution
\textbf{Strategies} (as in the text): $P_1^t$ trusts iff a bond was posted. $P_2^t$ posts a bond in period $t$ if the
guarantor has followed the contract in all earlier periods, and then cooperates; otherwise he does not post and
defects. The guarantor returns the bond iff $P_2^t$ cooperated.

\textbf{(1) $P_2^t$ cooperates, given the bond.} With the bond she gets $1 - w$ from $C$ and $2 - B - w$ from $D$
(she pays the fee in both cases, the bond is lost after $D$). Hence $C$ is optimal iff
\[
  B \ge 1 ,
\]
independently of $w$.

\textbf{(2) $P_2^t$ uses the guarantor.} Without a bond she would defect and $P_1^t$ would not trust, so she
gets $0$. With it she gets $1 - w$. She joins iff $w \le 1$ (strictly, $w < 1$).

\textbf{(3) The guarantor stays honest.} Following the contract earns $w$ every period; keeping the bond after a
cooperation earns $B$ once and then no one posts a bond again. He complies iff
\[
  \frac{w}{1-\delta} \ge B \iff w \ge (1-\delta)B .
\]
(The book's check is the same with $B = 2$, $w = 0.1$: $\frac{0.1}{1-\delta} \ge 2$, i.e.\ $\delta \ge 0.95$.)

\textbf{Choice for any $\delta$.} The three conditions need $B \ge 1$ (to avoid indifference take $B > 1$),
$(1-\delta)B \le w < 1$. For $B$ slightly above $1$ the interval $\bigl[(1-\delta)B,\,1\bigr)$ is non-empty for
\emph{every} $\delta > 0$, since $(1-\delta)B < 1$ when $B < \frac1{1-\delta}$. A concrete choice:
\[
  B = 1 + \frac\delta2, \qquad w = 1 - \frac\delta2 .
\]
Then $B > 1$ (cooperation is strictly optimal), $w < 1$ (she strictly prefers to use the guarantor, with surplus
$\frac\delta2$) and $\frac{w}{1-\delta} = \frac{1 - \delta/2}{1-\delta} > 1 + \frac\delta2 = B$ (the guarantor
strictly prefers to comply). Off the path nothing changes: after the guarantor cheats nobody posts a bond and
$(N)$, $D$ are stage equilibrium choices.

\interp The fee need only compensate the guarantor for the one-period gain from keeping a bond that is just
large enough to deter defection ($B$ close to $1$, the defector's gain). Making the bond small and the fee large
shrinks the guarantor's temptation relative to the stream of fees, so even very impatient guarantors are
honest. The limit is the fee ceiling $w < 1$ (what the clients are willing to pay) and $B \ge 1$.

\verify \texttt{check\_T3.py} (item~26) checks $\frac{0.1}{1-\delta} = 2$ at $\delta = 0.95$ and verifies the three
inequalities for $B = 1 + \frac\delta2$, $w = 1 - \frac\delta2$ at every $\delta = \frac{k}{100}$,
$k = 1,\dots,99$. Tadelis prints no answer.

\mistakes
\begin{itemize}
  \item Fixing the bond at $2$ and the fee at $0.1$: with those numbers $\delta \ge 0.95$ is needed.
  \item Charging a fee above $1$: $P_2^t$ then prefers no trade.
\end{itemize}

\answer{Take bond $B = 1 + \frac\delta2$ and fee $w = 1 - \frac\delta2$. Then $P_2^t$ cooperates ($B \ge 1$), joins the
guarantor ($w < 1$) and the guarantor complies ($\frac{w}{1-\delta} \ge B$), for every $\delta \in (0,1)$. In general
the conditions are $B \ge 1$, $w \le 1$ and $w \ge (1-\delta)B$.}
\end{exsol}
