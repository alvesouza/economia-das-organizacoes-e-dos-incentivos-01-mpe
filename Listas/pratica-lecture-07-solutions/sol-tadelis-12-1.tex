% Solution: Tadelis (2013) Exercise 12.1 "Chicken Revisited". Input by \solitem. Check: check_E1.py.
\begin{exsol}{Tadelis Exercise 12.1}{266}{Foundations}{2}
\asked Re-solve the chicken game of \S12.2.1 for $(R,H,L)=(8,16,0)$ and for $(8,0,16)$, and say
whether equilibrium analysis supports the claim that a society of lenient parents makes
teenagers behave worse.

\method Each kid has type $k\in\{L,H\}$ (cost of an accident), independent and equiprobable.
Payoffs of $i$: swerve ($C$) $=0$; drive ($D$) against $C$ gives $R$; $D$ against $D$ gives
$R/2-k$. Strategy $xy$: $x$ if the kid's parents are lenient ($L$), $y$ if harsh ($H$); build the
$4\times4$ Bayesian normal form and underline best replies (Tadelis pp.~252--255).

\solution
\textbf{Case $R=8,H=16,L=0$} (book's matrix, rows $CC,CD,DC,DD$, columns $cc,cd,dc,dd$):
\begin{center}\small
\begin{tabular}{l|cccc}
 & $cc$ & $cd$ & $dc$ & $dd$\\\hline
$CC$ & $0,0$ & $0,4$ & $0,4$ & $0,8$\\
$CD$ & $4,0$ & $-1,-1$ & $-1,3$ & $-6,2$\\
$DC$ & $4,0$ & $3,-1$ & $\br{3},\br{3}$ & $2,2$\\
$DD$ & $8,0$ & $2,-6$ & $2,2$ & $-4,-4$
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
The unique pure BNE is $(DC,dc)$: lenient kids ($k=0$) drive, harsh kids ($k=16$) swerve.
\emph{Preacher's claim:} in a society where \emph{all} parents are lenient, both kids have
$k=0$ and $D$ is strictly dominant ($4>0$ against $D$, $8>0$ against $C$): everybody drives and
the accident probability is $1$, against $\tfrac14$ in the mixed society (only lenient--lenient
pairs crash). The analysis supports the claim.

\textbf{Case $R=8,H=0,L=16$.} Now lenient parents impose the \emph{high} cost. Matrix:
\begin{center}\small
\begin{tabular}{l|cccc}
 & $cc$ & $cd$ & $dc$ & $dd$\\\hline
$CC$ & $0,0$ & $0,4$ & $0,4$ & $0,8$\\
$CD$ & $4,0$ & $\br{3},\br{3}$ & $3,-1$ & $2,2$\\
$DC$ & $4,0$ & $-1,3$ & $-1,-1$ & $-6,2$\\
$DD$ & $8,0$ & $2,2$ & $2,-6$ & $-4,-4$
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
The unique pure BNE is $(CD,cd)$: lenient kids swerve, harsh ($k=0$) kids drive ($D$ is dominant
for them). Check for a lenient kid: the rival drives with probability $\tfrac12$ at least, so
$D$ yields $\tfrac12\cdot 8+\tfrac12(4-16)=-2<0$. The prediction reverses: it is the
\emph{harsh}-parent kids who are aggressive. A society of all-lenient parents ($k=16$ for both)
is an ordinary chicken game with a symmetric mixed equilibrium in which each drives with
probability $q$ solving $8(1-q)+q(4-16)=0$, $q=\tfrac25$, so accidents have probability
$\tfrac4{25}<\tfrac14$, and the pure asymmetric equilibria have none. The claim is not supported.

\interp The conclusion is driven by the payoff story, not by the equilibrium concept: the same
model supports or reverses the preacher depending on whether leniency lowers or raises the
perceived cost of an accident. This is exactly the book's own caveat after the first matrix.

\verify \texttt{check\_E1.py} computes both $4\times4$ Bayesian normal forms from the types
(first case matches the printed entries $(CD,dd)=(-6,2)$ and $(DC,dc)=(3,3)$) and enumerates
all pure BNE: one each; it also checks $q=\tfrac25$. Tadelis gives no answer.

\answer{$(R,H,L)=(8,16,0)$: unique BNE $(DC,dc)$; the all-lenient society drives for sure, so the
claim is supported. $(8,0,16)$: unique BNE $(CD,cd)$ (harsh kids drive); the all-lenient society
is a chicken game with accident probability $\le\tfrac4{25}$; the claim is not supported.}
\end{exsol}
