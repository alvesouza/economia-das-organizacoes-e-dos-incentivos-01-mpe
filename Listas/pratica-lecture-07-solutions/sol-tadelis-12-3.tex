% Solution: Tadelis (2013) Exercise 12.3 "Armed Conflict". Input by \solitem. Check: check_E2.py.
\begin{exsol}{Tadelis Exercise 12.3}{266--267}{Exam}{3}
\asked All pure-strategy BNE of the armed-conflict game for (a) $m=3,w=2,s=1$ and (b) $m=3,w=4,s=2$.

\method Types $S$ or $W$ (equiprobable, independent), actions $A$ or $N$. An army's payoff from
$N$ is $0$. From $A$: $m$ if the rival plays $N$; if both attack, $m-c$ when $i$ is strong and the
rival weak, and $-c$ otherwise, where $c=s$ (strong) or $w$ (weak). A strategy is
$(x_S,x_W)$, the action of the strong and of the weak type. For each type compute the expected
payoff of $A$ against each of the four rival strategies and compare with $0$.

\solution
Expected payoff of $A$ for type $t$ against a rival strategy $(y_S,y_W)$ is
$\tfrac12 u(A\mid y_S\text{ vs }S)+\tfrac12 u(A\mid y_W\text{ vs }W)$; ($N$ yields $0$).

\textbf{(a) $m=3,w=2,s=1$.}
\begin{center}\small
\begin{tabular}{l|cccc}
rival $(y_S,y_W)$ & $AA$ & $AN$ & $NA$ & $NN$\\\hline
$E[A]$, strong & $\tfrac12$ & $1$ & $\tfrac52$ & $3$\\
$E[A]$, weak & $-2$ & $\tfrac12$ & $\tfrac12$ & $3$
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
The strong type attacks against every rival strategy ($E[A]>0$). The weak type attacks unless the
rival is $AA$ (always attacks). So a rival $AA$ forces $(A,N)$ from the other army, but against
$AN$ the weak type wants $A$: no symmetric pure equilibrium. The BNE are asymmetric:
\[
  (AA,\,AN)\quad\text{and}\quad(AN,\,AA).
\]
Check $(AA,AN)$: army~1 faces $AN$ and attacks with both types ($1>0$, $\tfrac12>0$); army~2 faces
$AA$, so its strong type attacks ($\tfrac12>0$) and its weak type stays ($-2<0$).

\textbf{(b) $m=3,w=4,s=2$.}
\begin{center}\small
\begin{tabular}{l|cccc}
rival $(y_S,y_W)$ & $AA$ & $AN$ & $NA$ & $NN$\\\hline
$E[A]$, strong & $-\tfrac12$ & $\tfrac12$ & $2$ & $3$\\
$E[A]$, weak & $-4$ & $-\tfrac12$ & $-\tfrac12$ & $3$
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
The weak type attacks only if the rival never attacks ($E[A]=3$ against $NN$, negative otherwise);
the strong type attacks unless the rival is $AA$. Profiles:
\[
  (AN,\,AN),\qquad (AA,\,NN),\qquad (NN,\,AA).
\]
Check $(AN,AN)$: strong attacks ($\tfrac12>0$ against $AN$), weak stays ($-\tfrac12<0$). $(AA,NN)$:
army~1 attacks with both types because army~2 never does ($3>0$), and army~2 stays with both types
because army~1 always attacks ($-\tfrac12<0$ and $-4<0$).

\interp (a) The costs are low enough that attacking pays unless the rival is sure to attack. The
only equilibria have one army whose weak type attacks and another whose weak type backs off.
(b) With high costs of fighting, only the strong type fights; the weak type attacks only against
a rival who never does, which supports the lopsided equilibria $(AA,NN)$ and $(NN,AA)$.

\verify \texttt{check\_E2.py} computes both Bayesian normal forms from the payoff rules and
enumerates all $4\times4$ pure profiles; it prints the two tables of expected attack payoffs above
and asserts exactly the two profiles in (a) and the three in (b). Tadelis gives no answer.

\answer{(a) $(AA,AN)$ and $(AN,AA)$. (b) $(AN,AN)$, $(AA,NN)$ and $(NN,AA)$; each strategy lists the
action of the strong type, then of the weak type.}
\end{exsol}
