# Practice plan — Aula 1: Does Organization Matter? Ford × GM × Toyota

20 items in one sequence of rising complexity: **14 existing book exercises** (Milgrom & Roberts
1992, Cabral 2017), cited by number and page only, plus **6 tasks on the class 1 companions**
(`Aula/aula-01-companions/`). Work them in order. Notes: [[Aula/aula-01-notes|Aula 1 notes]] ·
topic: [[rules/01_organizacoes_importam|Topic 1]] · index: [[Map/exercises-index|exercises-index]].

Every book reference was read in the PDF text layer on 2026-10-09 (M&R pp. 18, 52, 119–120;
Cabral pp. 11, 62–64). M&R prints two exercise sets per chapter: *Food for Thought* (FFT,
discursive) and *Mathematical Exercises* (Math). No answers are in either book. The companion
tasks are this course's own; their answers were checked numerically and are given at the end.

**C4 (companion task).** With every control except $A$ at its default, the specialized system is at
the corner $n = 1$. Using the closed forms on the page, write both profits as functions of $A$ and
find the value at which they tie. Check that both systems are still coherent there, then confirm
with the slider (step 1).

**C5 (companion task).** Keep the stand-alone values at $-4, -2, -1$. Argue, without the slider,
that no choice of the four interaction values lets any order avoid falling below Ford's corner.
Then find the smallest single change to one stand-alone value that lets some order climb to 111
without ever dropping below 10, and name that order.

**C6 (companion task).** In *The Complementarity Multiplier* at the defaults, compute the first
three rounds of the staircase by hand from $(t/c, s/c)$. Show that the distance to the optimum
shrinks by $(b/c)^2$ each round, and predict how many rounds it takes to get within 1% before
reading the page's "Rounds to within 1%".

## Companion answers (checked with `Aula/aula-01-companions/check_companions.py` and `model_two_systems.py`)

- **C1.** Profits by order: J→T→S 10, 6, 8, 16 · J→S→T 10, 6, 10, 16 · T→J→S 10, 8, 8, 16 · T→S→J 10, 8, 9, 16 · S→J→T 10, 9, 10, 16 · S→T→J 10, 9, 9, 16. Each first step adds one practice to an empty system, so its change is that practice's stand-alone value (−4, −2, −1); interactions need a partner already in place.
- **C2.** $b = 1$: multiplier $4/3$, $\partial y^*/\partial t = 1/3 > 0$. $b = 0$: multiplier 1, $\partial y^*/\partial t = 0$. $b = -1$: multiplier $4/3$, $\partial y^*/\partial t = -1/3 < 0$. The multiplier depends on $b^2$; the sign of $b$ only decides whether incentives move with delegation or against it.
- **C3.** At the defaults: specialized 1 model, batch 100, profit 100.0; flexible 4 models, batch 10, profit 87.7. *More taste for variety* ($A = 30$): flexible 9 models, profit 105.9, now best; specialized unchanged. *Cheaper flexibility* ($\Delta c = 0.85$): flexible profit 102.7, now best. Each system's location moves smoothly; which one is best jumps.
- **C4.** Specialized: $\pi_F = mQ - \sqrt{2 S_s Q h} = 200 - 100 = 100$, independent of $A$ while $2A^2 \le h S_s Q = 5000$. Flexible: $\pi_T = mQ + A\ln\!\big(2A^2/(h S_F Q)\big) - 2A - \Delta c\,Q = 100 + A\ln(A^2/100) - 2A$. Tie: $A \ln(A^2/100) = 2A \Rightarrow A^2/100 = e^2 \Rightarrow A = 10e \approx 27.18$. There $n_T = e^2 \approx 7.39 > n_s = 1.5625$ and $A < 50$, so both are coherent. On the slider the flexible system loses by 0.36 at $A = 27$ and wins by 1.66 at $A = 28$.
- **C5.** Every order's first step changes profit by one stand-alone value alone (C1), and all three are negative, so every order drops below 10 at step 1 whatever the interactions are (checked over the whole control grid: the best order's lowest point never exceeds 9). Raising $v_S$ from $-1$ to $0$ is enough: S→J→T then runs 10, 10, 11, 16.
- **C6.** From $(0.5, 0.5)$: round 1 $(0.75, 0.875)$, round 2 $(0.9375, 0.96875)$, round 3 $(0.984, 0.992)$, round 4 $(0.996, 0.998)$. The gap in $x$ goes $0.25, 0.0625, 0.0156, 0.0039$: factor $1/4 = (b/c)^2$. Within 1% needs 4 rounds, as the page reports.
